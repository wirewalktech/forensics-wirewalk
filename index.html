1

The argument this practice is built on

Forensic evidence is only worth what it survives. A finding nobody can reproduce gives the other side an opening. So does a fee that moves with the result, and so does a capability described without any mention of what it cannot do. The examiner who leaves one of those lying around is never the one who pays for it. The client is.

So I publish the things that get attacked instead of the things nobody can check. The method is written out in enough detail that an opposing examiner could reproduce it. The rate schedule is the one attached to the engagement letter: no discount, no promotion, no band. And there is a page on the limits of digital evidence, which comes before any description of what I can do rather than in small type after it.

What is missing matters as much. No case studies and no testimonials. No counters, no badge wall, and no suggestion that anything I produce is guaranteed to be admitted. Those are the parts of a forensics website that a cross-examiner reads back to the witness, one line at a time, and not one of them can be verified by the person reading it here.

1.1

The rate is the same whichever side retains me

The rate is fixed before I look at anything. It does not move with what I find, and it does not move according to which party instructs me. A null result is billed exactly as a helpful one: an examination that ends with me reporting that the artefacts do not support the proposition I was asked about costs the same $425/hr as one that supports it.

Examination and analysis are billed hourly against a ceiling you authorise in writing beforehand. At 80% of it I stop and report, and I do not resume without a written increase. That is an awkward thing to publish, because a ceiling is an admission that I cannot tell you the total in advance. An artefact of defined extent is different, and carries a fixed price instead: an image, a verified copy, a hash set. You can know the extent of those before the work starts. The full schedule, including testimony and the after-hours trigger, is on rates.

1.2

Every capability is published with what it does not establish

On scope, every line of work carries a column headed what this does not establish, and no cell in that column is empty. That is structural rather than a habit. To overclaim on this site I would have to leave a visible blank.

The reason is practical. The limits of a finding get written down somewhere, and the choice is only between writing them here, before the engagement, and writing them on the stand, under questioning, in front of the person who retained me. Timestamps mislead, and they do it routinely. A device is not a person. And the absence of an artefact is not evidence that the thing never happened. None of those three surprises anyone who does this work, and all three surprise almost everyone who commissions it.

2

What I take on, and what I am not

One principal. That is the whole practice. Where a matter needs a second pair of hands, or a capability I do not hold, I associate an examiner for that matter, name them in the engagement letter, and have them in place before the work that needs them begins. Where you see we on this site, that is what it means.

The work is acquisition and examination of computers, phones and cloud accounts. Incident response, at the size of organisation that has no incident response team. Review of an opposing examiner's report, declarations and expert support, and the collection, processing and production side of electronic discovery. The full ledger, line by line and marked take, refer out or decline, is on scope.

I am not an enterprise incident response consultancy and do not present myself as one. Regulated multi-jurisdiction breach response belongs to the panel firms with a bench, and so does full reverse engineering of a bespoke implant. I will work under or alongside them. Or I will say on the first call that I am the wrong firm, which costs nothing on the first call and costs the matter in week three.

Some engagements require a certified examiner, an accredited laboratory, or a licence for the venue. Where that is the case, the condition is identified at scoping and stated in the engagement letter, and it is settled before work begins in one of three ways: I already hold what that matter needs, or I work under someone who does, or I decline it. I check the licensing position for your venue rather than publishing a state-by-state table, because the map is contested and it moves.

3

Two lanes: something happening now, or a matter being prepared

Ransomware. A fraudulent wire. A departing employee whose laptop is already sitting in the reimage queue, or a device that has gone missing. If any of that is happening now, go to what to do in the first hour. That page is written to be used rather than read, it needs no scripts, and it is meant to be useful to someone who never calls me. Most avoidable evidence loss happens in that first hour, and it is nearly always done by competent people doing the job they were trained to do: containment carried out in a way that also does destruction.

The other lane is slower. A case that will need an examination, a production that has to survive a Rule 34 fight, an opposing report that needs answering: for those, the path runs through scope, rates, method and engagement. There is also a specimen report, marked throughout as a specimen and not a real matter, so you can see the shape of the deliverable before you commission one.

If nothing is on fire yet, that is the cheapest time to do this. A standby agreement runs the conflicts check against named adversaries, fixes the rate card for the year, and pre-agrees the terms and the out-of-hours authorisation while nobody is under pressure. It is the only circumstance in which I state a response commitment. Without one, what you have is an answer, and a quick yes or no on whether I can take the matter, including a straight no rather than a matter held while I think about it.

4

How an instruction begins

It starts with a scoping and preservation triage call. Up to 45 minutes, at no charge, with a written preservation instruction if one is needed. There is also no charge for the call that ends with me telling you the data cannot answer your question, or that you do not need a forensic examiner at all. I would much rather have that call than the alternative, which is billing you for work that was never going to help.

Then a conflicts check against the named parties, an engagement letter, and an opening authorisation with a stated ceiling. I do not start work, hold a date, or accept a single file before the conflicts check clears and the letter is signed. If you pay before that point the payment is an offer: it is held unapplied and refunded in full if the engagement does not proceed.

If a conflict surfaces mid-matter, work stops that day and the evidence is sequestered unexamined. The retaining party is told the same day. Material is returned or destroyed on instruction, and the unearned balance is refunded in full. Nothing I have learned goes to anyone, including to the party the conflict runs to. The mechanics, the invoicing terms and the authorisation ceilings are on engagement.

4.1

What not to send on the first call

Do not send case material, evidence, or privileged facts before the conflicts check has cleared. Send the parties' names, the question you need answered, the deadline and a callback number. A preliminary approach is logged for conflicts purposes even where it goes nowhere. Logging it creates no engagement, and what was said stays confidential.

There is no chat widget on this site and there will not be one. A chat bubble invites a caller to type privileged facts into a third-party service, which is an odd thing to install on a site about handling evidence carefully. The contact form collects a name, a role, whether counsel is engaged, and a number, with the reason for each field printed beside it. Nothing else.

6

What I will not do

This list is carried into the engagement letter, so it is a term you can hold me to rather than a statement on a website. It is a list of refusals of money and of method. It is not a list of things I cannot do; capability boundaries belong on scope, where they can be read as what they are.

I will not

  1. Take a fee that depends on what I find, on the outcome of the matter, or on which side retains me. The rate is the same either way and it is set before I look at anything. An examiner paid on the result is impeachable, and the client is the one who pays for that.
  2. Take or pay a referral fee, in either direction, to counsel, brokers, insurers, or anyone else who sends me work.
  3. Take a commission from any hardware, software, platform or hosting vendor I place, or sell you the remediation I was paid to investigate.
  4. Write to a conclusion supplied in advance, or report an inference as a finding because a stronger letter is wanted.
  5. Opine on who was at the keyboard from device evidence alone. A device is not a person.
  6. Opine that a party acted with intent to destroy evidence. I report what the artefacts show. Intent is the court's finding, not mine.
  7. Bill an hour above the ceiling you authorised in writing, or run an open-ended password recovery or extraction attempt without a written cap agreed first.
  8. Image a whole disk where a targeted collection answers the question, or bill you for expanded volume. Processing is measured on the data as I collected it.
  9. Tell you when culling would take your bill from five figures to four and then not do it, even though the bill I would otherwise send you is the larger one.
  10. Touch a device, mailbox or account without documented authority from someone entitled to give it, whatever the account holder's relationship to you, and whatever the story about ownership. That includes a spouse's phone, a former employee's personal account, and a saved session on a company laptop.
  11. Take a matter I cannot finish inside your deadline. I will say so on the first call rather than accept it and miss it.
  12. Start work, hold a date, or accept a single file before the conflicts check clears and the engagement letter is signed. A payment made before that point is an offer, held unapplied, and refunded in full.
  13. Carry an overage or surge rate. Hours beyond a standby credit bill at the retained rate, not above it.
  14. Deduct card processing fees from an unearned balance I am returning to you.
  15. Act for both sides in a matter, or act as an appointed neutral where I have advised either side.
  16. Take a matter that requires a certification, a licence or a qualification I do not hold. I identify that at scoping, name it in the engagement letter, and either bring in someone who holds it or decline.
  17. Promise you a finding, a recovered file, an unlocked handset, or that anything I produce will be admitted. Admissibility is the court's decision and nobody can guarantee it.
7

The venue, and the standard applied in it

I work in the New York area, where two different standards can apply to the same evidence. New York applies the Frye general-acceptance standard in its own courts. The federal courts sitting here apply FRE 702 as amended effective 1 December 2023, under which the proponent must show it is more likely than not that each requirement is met and that the opinion reflects a reliable application of the method to the facts of the case. Which standard applies is checked for your venue at engagement rather than assumed, and the answer changes what a report has to carry.

One thing follows from that, and it is worth knowing before you instruct anybody. The authentication foundation is built at collection time; it is not assembled later, before trial. That is why the acquisition log, the hash record and the chain of custody are written as the work happens rather than reconstructed afterwards. And it is why the questions an opposing examiner will ask are answered in the report itself, including the ones whose answers are unhelpful to the party who paid for it.

8

If you are deciding whether to forward this name

The honest summary is this. You would be sending a matter to one examiner in a small practice. I publish my rate and hold it. I write down the limits of a finding before I make it, and I will tell you on the first call if the work is beyond me or beyond the deadline. What you would not be forwarding it to is a consultancy with a bench, a laboratory with an accreditation, or anyone who will tell you your evidence is stronger than it is.

I report what the evidence supports, including when it supports nothing, or supports the other side. The fee is the same either way. That is a worse outcome for me than the alternative and a perfectly good one for you, and it is the whole of the argument.
