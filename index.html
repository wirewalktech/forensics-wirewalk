1

The argument this practice is built on

Forensic evidence is only worth what it survives. A finding that cannot be reproduced, a fee that moves with the result, a capability described without its limits — each of those is an opening for the other side, and each of them is paid for by the client rather than by the examiner who created it.

So this site publishes the things that get attacked, rather than the things that cannot be checked. The method is written out in enough detail that an opposing examiner could reproduce it. The rate schedule is the one attached to the engagement letter, with no discount, no promotion and no band. The limits of digital evidence are set out as a page in their own right, before any capability is described.

What is deliberately absent is as much of the point. There are no case studies, no testimonials, no counters, no badge wall, and no claim that anything we produce is guaranteed to be admitted. Those are the parts of a forensics website that a cross-examiner reads back to the witness, and none of them can be verified by the person reading them.

1.1

The rate is the same whichever side retains us

The rate is fixed before we look at anything, it does not move with what we find, and it does not move according to which party instructs us. A null result is billed exactly as a helpful one. An examination that ends with us reporting that the artefacts do not support the proposition we were asked about is billed at the same $425/hr as one that supports it.

Examination and analysis are billed hourly against a written ceiling you authorise in advance. We stop and report at 80% of it, and we do not resume without a written increase. Artefacts of defined extent — an image, an extraction, a verified copy, a hash set — carry a fixed price instead, because their extent is knowable before the work starts. The full schedule, including testimony and the after-hours trigger, is on rates.

1.2

Every capability is published with what it does not establish

On scope, every line of work carries a column headed what this does not establish, and no cell in that column is empty. That is a structural constraint rather than a habit: to overclaim on this site, someone would have to leave a visible blank.

The reason is practical. An examiner who has not written down the limits of a finding before the engagement will be writing them on the stand, under questioning, in front of the person who retained them. Timestamps mislead routinely. A device is not a person. Absence of an artefact is not evidence of absence. Those sentences are cheaper to read here than to hear for the first time in a deposition.

2

What the practice does, and what it is not

This is a small practice: one principal. Where a matter requires a second pair of hands, or a capability held by someone else, an examiner is associated for that matter, named in the engagement letter, and in place before the work that needs them begins. It takes computer, mobile and cloud acquisition and examination; incident response for the size of organisation that does not have an incident response team; review of an opposing examiner's report; declarations and expert support; and the collection, processing and production side of electronic discovery. The full ledger, line by line and marked take, refer out or decline, is on scope.

It is not an enterprise incident response consultancy and does not present itself as one. Regulated multi-jurisdiction breach response, and full reverse engineering of a bespoke implant, belong to the panel firms with a bench. We will work under or alongside them, or say on the first call that we are the wrong firm — which costs nothing on the first call and costs the matter in week three.

Where an engagement requires a certified examiner, an accredited laboratory, or a licence for the venue, that condition is identified at scoping, stated in the engagement letter, and satisfied before work begins — by holding it, by working under someone who holds it, or by declining the matter. We check the licensing position for your venue rather than publishing a state-by-state table; the map is contested and it moves.

3

Two lanes: something happening now, or a matter being prepared

If something is happening now — ransomware, a fraudulent wire, a departing employee whose laptop is in the reimage queue, a device gone missing — go to what to do in the first hour. That page is written to be used rather than read, needs no scripts, and is useful to someone who never calls us. Most avoidable evidence loss happens in that first hour, and it is nearly always done by competent people doing the job they were trained to do: containment carried out in a way that also does destruction.

Before you engage anyone, including us, call your insurer or broker. Policies commonly require notice within a short window measured from discovery, and commonly require the carrier's consent or the use of panel counsel and panel vendors; hiring outside that can reduce or void cover you have already paid for. Containment does not wait for the carrier. Procurement does. Our position on panels is stated on insurance.

If a matter is being prepared — a case that will need an examination, a production that must survive a Rule 34 fight, an opposing report that needs answering — the considered path runs through scope, rates, method and engagement. There is also a specimen report, marked throughout as a specimen and not a real matter, so you can see the shape of the deliverable before you commission one.

If nothing is on fire yet, that is the cheapest time to do this. A standby agreement runs the conflicts check against named adversaries, fixes the rate card for the year, and pre-agrees the terms and the out-of-hours authorisation while nobody is under pressure. It is the only circumstance in which this practice states a response commitment. Without one, the commitment is to answer, and to tell you quickly whether we can take the matter — including saying no immediately rather than holding it.

4

How an instruction begins

A scoping and preservation triage call, up to 45 minutes, at no charge, with a written preservation instruction if one is needed. There is also no charge for the call that ends with us telling you the data cannot answer your question, or that you do not need a forensic examiner at all. That call is common, and it is the shortest route to not being paid for work that would not have helped you.

Then a conflicts check against the named parties, an engagement letter, and an opening authorisation with a stated ceiling. We do not start work, hold a date, or accept a single file before the conflicts check clears and the letter is signed. A payment made before that point is an offer: it is held unapplied and refunded in full if the engagement does not proceed.

If a conflict surfaces mid-matter, work stops that day, the evidence is sequestered unexamined, the retaining party is told the same day, material is returned or destroyed on instruction, and the unearned balance is refunded in full. Nothing learned is disclosed to anyone, including to the party the conflict runs to. The mechanics, the invoicing terms and the authorisation ceilings are on engagement.

4.1

What not to send on the first call

Do not send case material, evidence, or privileged facts before the conflicts check has cleared. Send the parties' names, the question you need answered, the deadline, and a callback number. A preliminary approach is logged for conflicts purposes even where it goes nowhere; logging it creates no engagement, and what was said stays confidential.

There is no chat widget on this site and there will not be one, because a chat bubble invites a caller to type privileged facts into a third-party service. The contact form collects a name, a role, whether counsel is engaged, and a number — with the reason for each field printed beside it, and nothing else.

What we will not do

This list is carried into the engagement letter, so that it is a term you can hold us to rather than a statement on a website. It is a list of refusals of money and of method, not a list of things we cannot do — capability boundaries belong on scope, where they can be read as what they are.

We will not

  1. Take a fee that depends on what we find, on the outcome of the matter, or on which side retains us. The rate is the same either way and it is set before we look at anything. An examiner paid on the result is impeachable, and the client is the one who pays for that.
  2. Take or pay a referral fee, in either direction — to counsel, brokers, insurers, or anyone else who sends us work.
  3. Take a commission from any hardware, software, platform or hosting vendor we place, or sell you the remediation we were paid to investigate.
  4. Write to a conclusion supplied in advance, or report an inference as a finding because a stronger letter is wanted.
  5. Opine on who was at the keyboard from device evidence alone. A device is not a person.
  6. Opine that a party acted with intent to destroy evidence. We report what the artefacts show. Intent is the court's finding, not ours.
  7. Bill an hour above the ceiling you authorised in writing, or run an open-ended password recovery or extraction attempt without a written cap agreed first.
  8. Image a whole disk where a targeted collection answers the question, or bill you for expanded volume. Processing is measured on the data as we collected it.
  9. Tell you when culling would take your bill from five figures to four and then not do it — even though the bill we would otherwise send you is the larger one.
  10. Touch a device, mailbox or account without documented authority from someone entitled to give it, whatever the account holder's relationship to you, and whatever the story about ownership. That includes a spouse's phone, a former employee's personal account, and a saved session on a company laptop.
  11. Take a matter we cannot finish inside your deadline. We will say so on the first call rather than accept it and miss it.
  12. Start work, hold a date, or accept a single file before the conflicts check clears and the engagement letter is signed. A payment made before that point is an offer, held unapplied, and refunded in full.
  13. Carry an overage or surge rate. Hours beyond a standby credit bill at the retained rate, not above it.
  14. Deduct card processing fees from an unearned balance we are returning to you.
  15. Act for both sides in a matter, or act as an appointed neutral where we have advised either side.
  16. Take a matter that requires a certification, a licence or a qualification we do not hold. We identify that at scoping, name it in the engagement letter, and either bring in someone who holds it or decline.
  17. Promise you a finding, a recovered file, an unlocked handset, or that anything we produce will be admitted. Admissibility is the court's decision and no one can guarantee it.
7

The venue, and the standard applied in it

This practice is based in the New York area, where two different standards can apply to the same evidence. New York applies the Frye general-acceptance standard in its own courts, while the federal courts sitting here apply FRE 702 as amended effective 1 December 2023, under which the proponent must show it is more likely than not that each requirement is met and that the opinion reflects a reliable application of the method to the facts of the case. The applicable standard is checked for your venue at engagement rather than assumed, and the answer changes what a report has to carry.

One thing follows from that and is worth knowing before you instruct anyone: the authentication foundation is built at collection time, not assembled before trial. That is why the acquisition log, the hash record and the chain of custody are written as the work happens rather than reconstructed afterwards, and why the questions an opposing examiner will ask are answered in the report itself — including the ones whose answers are unhelpful to the party who paid for it.

8

If you are deciding whether to forward this name

The honest summary is this. You would be sending a matter to a small practice that publishes its rate and holds it, that writes down the limits of a finding before it makes one, and that will say on the first call if the work is beyond it or beyond the deadline. You would not be sending it to a consultancy with a bench, a laboratory with an accreditation, or a firm that will tell you your evidence is stronger than it is.

We report what the evidence supports, including when it supports nothing, or supports the other side. The fee is the same. That is a worse outcome for us and a perfectly good one for you, and it is the reason this is worth commissioning rather than taking our word for it.
