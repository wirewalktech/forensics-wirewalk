One rule

I do not sell the remediation for an incident I investigated. You get the findings in writing, in enough detail for anyone competent to act on, and someone else acts on them.

The work below is for the other case: nothing has happened, or what happened is closed.

2

What a forensics practice can tell you that a security review cannot

Most security assessments answer one question: could somebody get in. It is a good question and plenty of people answer it well.

I answer a different one, because it is the one I watch organisations fail. If something happened here on a Tuesday, could you prove what?

That failure is quiet and it is nearly universal. The logs that would have shown the intrusion were retained for thirty days and the intruder was inside for ninety. The mailbox audit setting that records which messages were actually read is a licence tier above the one you bought, so after a compromise you cannot establish what was exposed, and the notification decision gets made on assumption instead. Backups are excellent and nobody has ever restored from them. The departing employee's laptop is reimaged by a process that works exactly as designed.

None of that shows up in a penetration test. All of it decides whether you have a case, a notification obligation you can bound, or an insurance claim you can evidence.

4

What I do not do

Worth being blunt, because the category is full of people who will say yes to all of it.

  • I do not sell or resell security products, and I take no commission from anyone whose software or hardware I might mention. If a tool is the right answer I will name it and tell you I have no interest in whether you buy it.
  • I do not run a monitored service. Nobody here is watching your network at three in the morning. If you need that, you need a provider who does it, and I will say so.
  • I do not do penetration testing or offensive security. Different discipline, different qualifications, and there are people who do it properly.
  • I do not remediate what I investigated, for the reason in section 1.
  • I do not certify anyone as compliant with anything. I am not an auditor and this work carries no opinion.

Where a matter needs a certification, an accreditation or a licence I do not hold, I say so at scoping, name it in the engagement letter, and either bring in someone who holds it or decline.

5

Where to start

The evidence-readiness review, if you have no particular worry. It is the one that fails silently: you do not discover a thirty-day retention window is too short until the ninetieth day, and every other engagement here is better for knowing what your systems can actually tell you.

The insider-risk work, if the worry is specifically that somebody walks out with the client list. It starts with a bounded first block, and it usually ends with three or four changes that cost nothing.
