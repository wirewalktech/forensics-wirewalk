2

Matrimonial and family

This is the one where the law does more work than the technology, so I will start there.

I will not touch a device, an account or a mailbox without documented authority from someone entitled to give it. That holds whatever the relationship, whatever the story about who paid for the phone, and whatever is written on the bill. A saved session on a shared laptop is not authority. A password your spouse once told you is not authority. Being married is not authority.

I am careful about this because the exposure runs to the person who instructs me, not only to me. Unlawfully obtained material tends not merely to be excluded. It can taint the rest of the case, it can create a counterclaim, and in some circumstances accessing an account you are not entitled to access is a federal offence. People rarely arrive knowing that. I would rather say it on the first call than after.

What that leaves is a great deal, and it is usually what actually matters:

Matrimonial work
What you want to knowWhat I can usually establishWhat this does not establish
Whether assets have been moved or hidden Documents, spreadsheets, statements and communications on devices and accounts you are entitled to, with dates. Deleted files and superseded versions where the medium allows. The existence of an account nobody has disclosed. I read what is in front of me. Finding an undisclosed account is what disclosure and subpoenas are for.
What was said, and when Messages, mail and call records from lawfully held devices and accounts, reconstructed into a timeline and produced in a form the other side can test. That a particular person typed it. A device is not a person, and an account is not a person either.
Whether a device was wiped before handover Reinstall dates, wipe artefacts, the gap between last use and the state I received it in. Why. Intent is the court's finding, not mine. I describe what the artefacts show and stop there.
Whether a phone is being tracked, or a device has stalkerware on it Installed applications, profiles, device management, and permissions on a handset you own and control. Who installed it, unless the artefacts happen to say. Often they do not.
What a court has ordered produced Forensic preservation and production of exactly what the order covers, with the custody record that makes it usable, and nothing outside it. Anything outside the order. Over-collection in a family matter is a problem, not thoroughness.

Instructed through counsel wherever possible. It is not a rule, but the work is more useful when it is done under privilege and to a defined question.

3

An employee is leaving, or has already gone

The most time-critical matter that is not an emergency, and the one where the damage is almost always done by ordinary helpfulness rather than by anybody's bad intent.

The laptop goes into the return queue. IT reimages it on Thursday because that is the process, and the process is good. The mailbox is deleted on day thirty by an offboarding script nobody has looked at in two years. The cloud drive empties itself. None of this is anyone's fault, and all of it is irreversible.

Take the laptop out of that queue today. Not next week. Label it, log who has it, put it in a drawer, and do not boot it. Booting alters thousands of timestamps, can trigger a cloud sync that deletes the very files you are asking about, and on a managed device can trigger a remote wipe. Put the same hold on the mailbox, the cloud drive and the home directory, all of which delete on a timer.

Then decide what you actually want to know, because the artefacts differ:

Departing-employee work
The questionWhere the answer livesWhat this does not establish
Was a USB drive connected, and when Registry device keys and setup logs, giving make, serial and first and last connection. That anything was copied to it. Connection is not copying. This is the single most over-read artefact in the field.
Were files copied to removable media The change journal, link files, recent-items and shellbags, read together rather than alone. Certainty, on its own. A copy finding is built from several artefacts agreeing. One artefact is an argument, not a finding.
Were files sent to personal mail or personal cloud Mail logs, browser history and download records, sync client logs where the client was installed. What was inside an attachment the mail system no longer holds.
Was anything deleted before handover Deletion artefacts, recycle bin records, shadow copies where they survive. Whether it was deliberate. Again: intent is for the court.
Did they take the client list Access and export events in the CRM or file server logs, which are usually the better evidence and are usually forgotten. What they remember. Nothing on a disk reaches what is in somebody's head, and that is often the real question.

Do not tip them off, and know what does the tipping. An abrupt account disable, a device recall, a new block on something they use daily, a meeting that does not fit the calendar. When and how the person is told is a decision for counsel and for HR, driven by the employment agreement and the law where they work. It is not an IT decision and it is not mine.

I will also say the unwelcome thing early, because it saves money: quite often the artefacts show ordinary behaviour that looks alarming when you go looking for alarm. People email themselves documents. People plug in drives. If that is what it turns out to be, I will tell you that, and the engagement ends there rather than expanding.

4

Employment disputes

Termination, discrimination, harassment at work, a grievance that has become a claim. The evidence is usually mail, chat and access logs, and it usually exists on systems the employer controls, which changes the practical question from "can it be recovered" to "has it been preserved and can it be produced in a defensible form".

Acting for an employer, the risk is deleting something after a duty to preserve has attached. Acting for an individual, the risk is the opposite: the material sits on a system you do not control and is disappearing while the parties argue. Either way the first useful step is a preservation letter and a hold, not an examination.

Where a matter needs a certification, an accreditation or a licence I do not hold, I say so at scoping, name it in the engagement letter, and either bring in someone who holds it or decline.

5

Business and partnership disputes

A co-founder, a partner, a departing shareholder. What makes these awkward is that the systems are shared, so "who is entitled to authorise an examination of this mailbox" is a live question rather than a formality, and getting it wrong contaminates the work.

I want that settled in writing before I touch anything, and I would rather lose the instruction than proceed on somebody's assurance that they have the authority. Where the entity itself is the client, that is usually clean. Where one individual is instructing about a system the entity controls, it usually is not.

6

Harassment, threats and stalking

Anonymous messages, a fake profile, somebody who knows things they should not. Two honest notes about this work.

First, attribution is hard and is often impossible from the content alone. What usually breaks these cases is a subpoena to a platform or a carrier, which is a legal instrument rather than a technical one, and which your lawyer obtains. What I can do is preserve the material properly so the subpoena has something to point at, document it in a form the police or a court will accept, and tell you which of the available routes has any chance of working.

Second, if you are in danger, the order of operations is the police first and me second. I am not a substitute for that, and nothing on this page is.

7

Estate and probate disputes

A device nobody can open, a will that may have been altered, transfers made in the months before a death. The work is the same as anywhere else; the authority question is the one that differs, because the person who could have authorised access is the person who died.

The executor or administrator is usually the right source of authority, and usually the only one. Ask me before the family tries the passcode. Repeated guesses lock some devices permanently, and a small number wipe.

8

What is the same in all of them

Authority before access. Preserve before you examine. State the limits of a finding at the same time as the finding. And the answer is sometimes that the evidence cannot settle the question, which is a real answer and one I would rather give you in week one than in week six.
