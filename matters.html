Matrimonial and family

Authority first, and it protects you more than me. I touch no device, account or mailbox without documented authority from someone entitled to give it. A saved session on a shared laptop is not authority. A password your spouse once told you is not authority. Being married is not authority.

Unlawfully obtained material is not merely excluded. It can taint the case, create a counterclaim, and in some circumstances accessing an account you are not entitled to access is a federal offence. What is left is usually what matters:

Matrimonial work
What you want to knowWhat I can usually establishDoes not establish
Whether assets have been moved or hidden Dated documents, statements and communications on devices and accounts you are entitled to; deleted and superseded versions where the medium allows. An account nobody has disclosed. That is what subpoenas are for.
What was said, and when Messages, mail and call records from lawfully held devices, built into a timeline the other side can test. That a particular person typed it. A device is not a person.
Whether a device was wiped before handover Reinstall dates, wipe artefacts, and the gap between last use and the state I received it in. Why. Intent is the court's finding, not mine.
Whether a phone is tracked, or carries stalkerware Installed applications, profiles, device management and permissions on a handset you control. Who installed it, unless the artefacts happen to say.
What a court has ordered produced Preservation and production of exactly what the order covers, with the custody record that makes it usable. Anything outside the order. Over-collection here is a problem.

You end up holding the image, the exhibit set and a written report with its limits stated. Instructed through counsel wherever possible: the work is more useful under privilege.

An employee is leaving, or has already gone

Take the laptop out of the return queue today, and do not boot it. Booting alters thousands of timestamps, can trigger a cloud sync that deletes the files you are asking about, and on a managed device can trigger a remote wipe. Label it, log who has it, put it in a drawer. Hold the mailbox, cloud drive and home directory too — all three delete on a timer.

None of that loss is anyone's fault. IT reimages on Thursday because that is the process, the offboarding script runs on day thirty, and all of it is irreversible.

Departing-employee work
The questionWhere the answer livesDoes not establish
Was a USB drive connected, and when Registry device keys and setup logs: make, serial, first and last connection. That anything was copied to it. Connection is not copying.
Were files copied to removable media The change journal, link files, recent items and shellbags, read together rather than alone. Certainty from one artefact. A copy finding is several artefacts agreeing.
Were files sent to personal mail or cloud Mail logs, browser history, download records, and sync client logs where one was installed. What was inside an attachment the mail system no longer holds.
Was anything deleted before handover Deletion artefacts, recycle bin records, shadow copies where they survive. Whether it was deliberate.
Did they take the client list Access and export events in CRM or file server logs — the better evidence, usually forgotten. What they remember. No disk reaches what is in somebody's head.

Do not tip them off, and know what does the tipping. An abrupt account disable, a device recall, a new block on something they use daily. When the person is told is a decision for counsel and HR, not for IT and not for me.

The unwelcome thing, said early because it saves money: often the artefacts show ordinary behaviour that looks alarming because you went looking for alarm. People email themselves documents. If that is what it is, I tell you, and the engagement ends there.

Employment disputes

Termination, discrimination, harassment at work, a grievance that has become a claim. The evidence is mail, chat and access logs on systems the employer controls, so the question is not "can it be recovered" but "was it preserved, and can it be produced defensibly".

For an employer, the risk is deleting something after a duty to preserve attached. For an individual, it is material ageing out on a system you do not control. Either way the first step is a preservation letter and a hold, not an examination.

Where a matter requires a certification, an accreditation or a licence I do not hold, I say so at scoping, name it in the engagement letter, and either bring in someone who holds it or decline.

Business and partnership disputes

A co-founder, a partner, a departing shareholder. The systems are shared, so "who can authorise an examination of this mailbox" is a live question rather than a formality, and getting it wrong contaminates the work.

I want it settled in writing before I touch anything, and I would rather lose the instruction than proceed on an assurance. Where the entity is the client, that is usually clean. Where one individual instructs about a system the entity controls, it usually is not.

Harassment, threats and stalking

Anonymous messages, a fake profile, somebody who knows things they should not. Attribution from content alone is often impossible; what breaks these cases is a subpoena to a platform or a carrier, which your lawyer obtains.

I preserve the material so the subpoena has something to point at, in a form the police or a court will accept, and tell you which routes have any chance of working. If you are in danger, police first and me second.

Estate and probate disputes

A device nobody can open, a will that may have been altered, transfers in the months before a death. The work is the same as anywhere else; the authority question differs, because the person who could have authorised access is the one who died. The executor or administrator is usually the only right source.

Ask me before the family tries the passcode. Repeated guesses lock some devices permanently, and a small number wipe.

What holds in all of them

Authority before access. Preserve before you examine. The limits of a finding stated alongside the finding. And sometimes the evidence cannot settle the question — a real answer, better given in week one than week six.
