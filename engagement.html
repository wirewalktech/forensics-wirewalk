1

The order things happen in

A scoping call, then a conflicts check, then an engagement letter with the rate schedule and a written authorisation ceiling attached, then an advance deposit, then work. Each step finishes before the next one starts, and the order is not negotiable under time pressure. An examination that begins before conflicts have cleared is an examination that can be taken away from the party who commissioned it.

State it plainly, because the rest of this page depends on it: there is no engagement until the conflicts check clears and the engagement letter is signed. Until both are true, nothing is retained, no date is held, and no evidence is accepted.

1.1

What does not create an engagement

A telephone call does not. An email describing the matter does not. Nor does a meeting, an introduction from counsel, a signed non-disclosure agreement, or a request that we hold a week in March.

A payment does not either. Money received before the check clears and the letter is signed is an offer: held unapplied, no work done against it, refunded in full if the engagement does not proceed, with no deduction for card processing. We would rather return money than have the first exhibit in a fee dispute be an invoice for work done before anyone was retained.

2

Conflicts

The check runs before anything else, and before we hear anything we would rather not have heard. We ask for the names first and the story second: if a conflict exists, the less we have been told at the point it surfaces, the cleaner the withdrawal.

We do not act for both sides in a matter, and we do not accept an appointed-neutral role where we have advised either side. Where a relationship is not a conflict but a reasonable person would want to know about it, it is disclosed in writing before the letter is signed and you decide.

2.1

What we ask for to run it

Full legal names of the parties, including parent and subsidiary entities; opposing counsel; the individuals likely to be custodians or subjects; co-defendants, insurers and third parties you expect to be joined; and the venue. Trade names are not enough — a check against a trading name alone does not find a position held against a differently registered subsidiary of the same group.

If the list is not complete yet, say so. We can run a partial check and tell you it is partial. What we will not do is proceed on a list we know to be incomplete without saying so in writing.

2.2

What is logged, and what logging is not

Every preliminary approach is logged for conflicts purposes, including the approaches that go nowhere. The log records the date, the parties named, the venue, the general subject-matter, and the outcome — engaged, declined, no response. It exists so that a later approach from the other side in the same matter is recognised as one.

Logging an approach creates no engagement. It is a record, not a retainer. It obliges us to nothing and it obliges you to nothing.

What was said on a preliminary call stays confidential. We do not repeat it, we do not use it, and the fact that you called is not disclosed to the other side or to anyone else — whether or not you instruct us, and whether or not we decline.

We know why the log has to exist: parties do call specialists early in order to put them out of reach of the other side. A logged approach is not a conflict by itself, and we do not treat a five-minute call as a lifetime disqualification.

2.3

If a conflict surfaces after work has started

A party is joined. An entity turns out to belong to a group named elsewhere in the conflicts log. A custodian name matches one already logged. The protocol is written down in advance because it is not a decision anyone should make for the first time on the day.

Mid-matter conflict protocol
StepWhat happensWhat this does not establish
1 Work stops at the point the conflict is recognised. No further examination, nothing further written. Does not mean the evidence already collected is unreliable. It means we do no further work on it.
2 Evidence in our custody is sequestered unexamined: sealed, access-logged, not opened again except to return or destroy it on written instruction. Does not undo work already reported before the conflict surfaced. That work exists and we say so.
3 The retaining party is told the same day, in writing, what was found and when. Does not describe another party's matter. The disclosure names the conflict, nothing more.
4 Material is returned or destroyed on your instruction, with a certificate where destruction is chosen. Does not relieve you of your own preservation obligations. Returning our copy is not a reason to stop preserving the source.
5 The unearned balance is refunded in full within 10 business days, with no deduction for processing or administration. Does not cover work performed and reported before the conflict was recognised, which is billed at the authorised rate.
6 Nothing learned in the matter is disclosed to anyone — including, specifically, the party the conflict runs to. Does not depend on a later request or on the other party's instructions. If we are ordered to produce, you are told before we do.
3

Who the client is

The client is whoever signs the engagement letter and is named in it, and that is not always the party whose data we examine. Where counsel instructs on behalf of a company, counsel is the client: the letter runs to the firm, instructions come from the named lawyer, the report is delivered to the firm, and the firm decides who else sees it. The company is the party in interest, not the instructing party, and the distinction governs where every deliverable goes.

That structure is usually chosen for privilege reasons. It is your lawyer's decision, not ours, and we will paper it either way. What we will not tell you is that involving us makes anything privileged; the honest position is on /roles, with the four instruction roles and how discovery obligations differ between them. Where a company instructs directly, the company is the client; where an individual instructs in a personal matter, the individual is. The letter names one client, and only one.

3.1

Instructions, and who may give them

The letter names the people entitled to instruct and to authorise an increase in the ceiling. Anything that changes the scope, the ceiling or the deliverable is accepted in writing only; a request by telephone is confirmed by email before it is acted on. An unwritten instruction to widen a search is the most common source of a billing dispute in this work.

Material supplied by counsel is segregated and logged separately from material we collected ourselves, so that the carve-outs at FRCP 26(b)(4)(C) — compensation, facts or data provided by counsel and considered, and assumptions provided and relied on — can be honoured without sweeping in protected communications or draft reports, which are protected as trial-preparation material under FRCP 26(b)(4)(B).

4

Intake, and what not to send yet

Intake is short by design: your name, your role, whether counsel is engaged, and a callback number. We do not ask for case detail in a web form, and there is no chat widget on this site, because a chat bubble invites a caller to type privileged facts into a third party's service.

Before the engagement letter is signed, please do not send:

  • Evidence. No drives, phones or laptops, no exported mailboxes, no images, no single loose file. Material arriving before an engagement exists is held sealed and unexamined or returned unopened, and either way it has been moved for no reason.
  • Privileged or confidential documents. Send the question, not the file.
  • Credentials. No passwords, tokens, recovery keys or administrator accounts. There is no point in this process at which we ask for a password by email, and an email asking for one is not from us.
  • Regulated data. Protected health information, cardholder data, controlled unclassified information, or personal data under GDPR or UK GDPR. These carry handling constraints settled at scoping, before anything moves.

What is useful before signature: the names for the conflicts check, your deadline, the question in one sentence, and an inventory of what exists — how many devices, roughly how much data, which platforms, and whether anything is already under a preservation obligation. None of that is evidence, and it is enough to scope and price.

4.1

Documented authority to examine a device or an account

Before any device, mailbox or account is touched, we confirm in writing who is authorising the examination, on what basis, and for which specific item. This is required whatever the story about ownership, and it is the condition most often resisted at intake.

It applies to a company laptop in a departing employee's possession, a personal phone used for work, a shared household machine, a spouse's device, a former employee's personal mailbox, and a saved session still logged into someone's personal account. "It is our laptop" answers a question about the hardware, not the question about the account.

The reasons are statutory, not decorative: the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1030; the Stored Communications Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2702, which is why a civil subpoena to a provider does not produce message content; the Wiretap Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2511; and state computer-crime and eavesdropping statutes, which are not uniform. Where authority is unclear we stop and ask. Where it cannot be established we decline that item in writing, rather than examine it and leave the problem for cross-examination.

Suspected child sexual abuse material. This practice does not take or retain custody of it. If it is encountered or anticipated we stop, escalate through counsel, and work through law enforcement on access, noting 18 U.S.C. § 3509(m) in federal criminal matters. This cannot be agreed away in an engagement letter, and clients are told before they instruct us rather than afterwards. Full procedure on /evidence-handling.
5

Authorisation ceilings

Analysis is billed hourly against a ceiling authorised in writing before work starts. The ceiling is a number, attached to a defined phase, and no hour above it is billed — not as an overage, not at a reduced rate, not as a courtesy line that appears on the invoice anyway.

We propose the opening authorisation ourselves, with the reasoning: what we expect to look at first, what that should answer, and what would make it take longer. We publish no "typical hours" figure, because that would be a claim about past matters. What we publish instead is the opening authorisation we would propose, which you can compare against anyone else's.

5.1

The stop-and-report at 80 per cent

At 80% of an authorised ceiling, work stops and you receive a written report: hours consumed, what has been established, what remains unexamined, what the remaining work would cost, and whether we think it is worth doing. Work resumes only on a written increase. Silence is not an increase and neither is a verbal one.

The report at 80% is also where we tell you if the answer is not going to be there. Stopping at four-fifths of a ceiling is a worse commercial outcome for us than running to the end of it, which is exactly why the stop-point is published rather than left to judgement on the day.

5.2

Which class a line falls into

A fixed price buys an artefact of defined extent — an image, an extraction, a copy, a hash set, a certificate. Analysis cannot be priced before someone has looked at the artefact, because the extent of an answer is not knowable in advance. That is why some lines on /rates carry a price and some carry a rate and a ceiling.

Price classes
ClassHow it is authorisedWhat this does not establish
Fixed A stated price for a stated unit, named in the letter. Does not cover analysis of what was acquired, and does not guarantee the artefact answers your question. An image is a copy, not a finding.
Hourly against a ceiling A published rate plus a written stop-point, with the report at 80%. Does not predict the hours. A ceiling caps exposure; it does not tell you what the work will cost.
Scoped after paid triage A fixed, time-boxed first block, credited in full against whatever follows. Does not commit you to the follow-on work, or us to a price for it before the triage output exists.

There is no overage rate and no surge rate. Hours beyond a standby credit bill at the retained rate, not above it.

6

Invoicing

Invoices are issued monthly and on completion of a phase, and each one reconciles against the authorisation: hours billed, hours remaining under the ceiling, deposit balance after application. An invoice that cannot be reconciled against an authorisation you signed is our error, and it is withdrawn.

Time is recorded in increments of 0.1 hour, one task per entry. There is no block billing. An entry names the date, the timekeeper, the task, the data source it relates to, and the duration. A specimen entry, in the format the invoice uses: 2026-09-14 · principal · keyword search across mailbox export MBX-02 · 1.4 — not "review and analysis, 6.0". The reason is not tidiness: an invoice in this work becomes an exhibit, and a block-billed entry cannot be defended line by line in a fee application or under questioning about what was actually done.

Expenses are billed at cost with no mark-up, with receipts. Travel is billed at the published travel rate and capped per day. Where after-hours or expedited work triggers the uplift, only the out-of-hours hours carry it, and those hours appear separately so the uplift can be checked rather than taken on trust.

6.1

Invoice formats and e-billing portals

What we produce as standard: an itemised PDF invoice, and the same detail as a CSV time export, one row per entry, which loads into a spreadsheet or a matter-management system without rekeying.

Electronic-billing portals are a separate question. Portal formats — LEDES among them — impose their own field structure, task and activity coding, and submission rules.

The invoice format, any task-code set, and the submission route are agreed in writing before the first invoice is raised. Where your portal requires a format we cannot produce, we say so at intake and agree an alternative, or you use another route. We do not discover this at the first submission.

If a portal is mandated, tell us at intake and send the specification. Sixty days of non-payment because an invoice was rejected for a coding fault is avoidable in one conversation before the matter starts.

6.2

Purchase orders and vendor onboarding

Where a purchase order is required it must exist before work starts, and its number goes on the engagement letter — an invoice arriving without one in a corporate accounts-payable system does not get paid, it gets returned, usually silently. If the purchase order carries a value lower than the authorised ceiling, the purchase order value governs and we stop there.

Vendor onboarding typically asks for a vendor form, a W-9, banking details, supplier terms or a master services agreement, security questionnaires, and evidence of insurance at stated limits.

Tell us at intake what your onboarding requires. Each requirement — including any certificate of insurance at your stated limits — is answered individually before a vendor record is opened, and where one cannot be met you are told at that point rather than at the first invoice. We do not answer an onboarding questionnaire by attesting to something we have not verified.

Banking details are confirmed by telephone to a number you already hold and they do not change mid-matter. An email that appears to come from us changing our payment details is not from us. Call first.

6.3

Payment terms

Invoices are payable within 30 days of issue. Bank transfer is preferred above a threshold stated in the engagement letter, and carries a 3% discount against the card price. That is a payment-method differential, not a discount on the rate schedule, which carries none. It is not a card surcharge either — a surcharge is a materially different thing, is unlawful in several states, and cannot lawfully be applied to a debit card at all.

Pay-when-paid terms are not accepted, in any form — not on receipt of funds from your client, not on settlement, not at the conclusion of the matter. The reason is the one that governs the rate: a fee contingent on an outcome, or on somebody else's recovery, makes the examiner's compensation an issue at trial, and the client is the one who pays for that.

7

Retainer mechanics

An advance deposit is taken before work starts and applied against invoices as they issue. The mechanics are published here in the same words the engagement letter uses, because retainer handling is where a small practice most easily says something it is not entitled to say.

  • The deposit is held in the practice's operating account as an advance deposit against fees. It is not a trust account, not escrow and not IOLTA, and it is never described as any of those here, in a letter, or on an invoice. Those words carry meanings belonging to regulated professions, and an examiner borrowing them is describing a protection that does not exist.
  • No interest accrues to the payer on a held balance.
  • The unearned balance is refunded in full within 10 business days of matter close, measured from the final invoice. No processing fee, no administration fee, and a card-paid deposit is refunded at full face value. We do not keep the card processor's cut out of your money.
  • No automatic replenishment. A card is not charged again without a separately signed authorisation naming the trigger, the amount and a ceiling. A card on file is not permission to top up a balance.
  • A deposit is not an authorisation. Money held authorises no hour of work; only a written ceiling does. A balance larger than the work you authorised does not quietly become permission for more.
8

Closing a matter

A matter closes on a final invoice. The unearned balance is refunded on the terms at section 7; evidence storage continues for 90 days at no charge and then at the published monthly rate until you instruct otherwise; material is returned or destroyed on written instruction, with a certificate issued on destruction.

Nothing is destroyed without a written instruction, and nothing is destroyed while we have notice of a preservation obligation, a litigation hold or a subpoena touching the material. Where compulsory process reaches material we hold, you are notified so that you can object, unless we are prohibited by law from telling you. The custody, storage and retention terms are on /evidence-handling.

9

The refusals that bind an engagement

The full list is at /#we-will-not and is repeated verbatim in every engagement letter. The clauses below are the ones bearing directly on how an instruction is taken and billed, reproduced word for word from that list; the numbering there is the numbering to cite.

We will not — extract

  1. Take a fee that depends on what we find, on the outcome of the matter, or on which side retains us. The rate is the same either way and it is set before we look at anything. An examiner paid on the result is impeachable, and the client is the one who pays for that.
  2. Bill an hour above the ceiling you authorised in writing, or run an open-ended password recovery or extraction attempt without a written cap agreed first.
  3. Touch a device, mailbox or account without documented authority from someone entitled to give it, whatever the account holder's relationship to you, and whatever the story about ownership. That includes a spouse's phone, a former employee's personal account, and a saved session on a company laptop.
  4. Take a matter we cannot finish inside your deadline. We will say so on the first call rather than accept it and miss it.
  5. Start work, hold a date, or accept a single file before the conflicts check clears and the engagement letter is signed. A payment made before that point is an offer, held unapplied, and refunded in full.
  6. Carry an overage or surge rate. Hours beyond a standby credit bill at the retained rate, not above it.
  7. Deduct card processing fees from an unearned balance we are returning to you.
  8. Act for both sides in a matter, or act as an appointed neutral where we have advised either side.
  9. Take a matter that requires a certification, a licence or a qualification we do not hold. We identify that at scoping, name it in the engagement letter, and either bring in someone who holds it or decline.
  10. Promise you a finding, a recovered file, an unlocked handset, or that anything we produce will be admitted. Admissibility is the court's decision and no one can guarantee it.

Where a matter requires a certified examiner, an accredited laboratory, or a licence in the venue, that condition is identified at scoping, stated in the engagement letter, and satisfied before work begins. We check the licensing position for the venue and either hold the licence, work under one, or decline the matter.
