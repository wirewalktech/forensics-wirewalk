1

The order things happen in

A scoping call, then a conflicts check, then an engagement letter with the rate schedule and a written authorisation ceiling attached, then an advance deposit, then work. Each step finishes before the next one starts. That holds when you are in a hurry, which is most of the time, and it is the part of this page people argue with first. An examination that begins before conflicts have cleared is an examination that can be taken away from the party who commissioned it.

So, plainly, because the rest of this page rests on it: there is no engagement until the conflicts check clears and the engagement letter is signed. Until both of those are true I am not retained, I am not holding a date for you, and I will not take custody of anything.

1.1

What does not create an engagement

A phone call does not. Nor does an email describing the matter, a meeting, an introduction from counsel, a signed non-disclosure agreement, or asking me to keep a week in March free.

Payment does not either. Money that arrives before the check clears and the letter is signed is an offer. I hold it unapplied, do no work against it, and refund it in full if the engagement does not proceed, without deducting what the card processor took. I would rather give money back than have the first exhibit in a fee dispute be my own invoice for work done before I was anyone's examiner.

1.2

What each document fixes, and what it does not

I narrow the conflicts check to the parties named on the call and run it before the first hour of work rather than alongside it, and the letter becomes a short form signed electronically. What changes is the elapsed time, not the order. If a conflict turns up after work has begun, section 2.3 applies in full, refund included. What to do in the first hour is on /emergency, whether or not you call me.
2

Conflicts

I run the check before anything else, and before I have heard anything I would rather not have heard. Names first, story second. If a conflict exists, the less I have been told when it surfaces, the cleaner the withdrawal, and the shorter the argument about what I now know.

I do not act for both sides in a matter, and I will not take an appointed-neutral role where I have advised either side. Some relationships are not conflicts but a reasonable person would still want to hear about them. Those go to you in writing before the letter is signed, and you decide.

2.1

What I ask for to run it

Full legal names of the parties, including parent and subsidiary entities; opposing counsel; the individuals likely to be custodians or subjects; co-defendants, insurers and third parties you expect to be joined; and the venue. Trade names are not enough. A check against a trading name alone does not find a position held against a differently registered subsidiary of the same group, which sounds like pedantry until the day it catches something.

If the list is not complete yet, say so. I can run a partial check and tell you in writing that it is partial. What I will not do is run one against a list I know to be incomplete and let you believe it was the whole thing.

2.2

What is logged, and what logging is not

I log every preliminary approach for conflicts purposes, including the ones that go nowhere. The log records the date, the parties named, the venue, the general subject-matter and the outcome: engaged, declined, no response. It exists so that when the other side in the same matter calls, long after the first approach, I recognise it as the same matter.

Logging an approach creates no engagement. It is a record, not a retainer. It obliges neither of us to anything.

What was said on a preliminary call stays confidential. I do not repeat it and I do not use it. The fact that you called is not disclosed to the other side or to anyone else, whether or not you instruct me, and whether or not I am the one who declines.

I am aware of what a log like that can be used for. Parties do call specialists early precisely to put them out of the other side's reach. So a logged approach is not a conflict by itself, and a five-minute call is not a lifetime disqualification.

2.3

If a conflict surfaces after work has started

A party gets joined. An entity turns out to belong to a group named elsewhere in the log. A custodian's name matches one I already have. It happens, and the protocol below is written down in advance because the day it happens is a bad day to be working out what to do.

Mid-matter conflict protocol
StepWhat happensWhat this does not establish
1 I stop at the point I recognise the conflict. No further examination, nothing further written. Does not mean the evidence already collected is unreliable. It means I do no further work on it.
2 Evidence in my custody is sequestered unexamined: sealed, access-logged, not opened again except to return or destroy it on written instruction. Does not undo work already reported before the conflict surfaced. That work exists, and I say so.
3 I tell the retaining party the same day, in writing, what I found and when. Does not describe another party's matter. The disclosure names the conflict, nothing more.
4 Material is returned or destroyed on your instruction, with a certificate where destruction is chosen. Does not relieve you of your own preservation obligations. Returning my copy is not a reason to stop preserving the source.
5 I refund the unearned balance in full within 10 business days, with nothing deducted for processing or administration. Does not cover work performed and reported before the conflict was recognised, which is billed at the authorised rate.
6 Nothing I learned in the matter goes to anyone else, and that includes, specifically, the party the conflict runs to. Does not depend on a later request or on the other party's instructions. If I am ordered to produce, you hear it from me before I do.
3

Who the client is

My client is whoever signs the engagement letter and is named in it, and that is not always the party whose data I examine. Where counsel instructs on behalf of a company, counsel is the client: the letter runs to the firm, instructions come from the named lawyer, the report goes to the firm, and the firm decides who else sees it. The company is the party in interest rather than the instructing party. That sounds like a formality, and it is not. It decides where every deliverable goes, including the ones the company's own IT director would very much like a copy of.

The structure is usually chosen for privilege reasons. That is your lawyer's decision and not mine, and I will paper it either way. What I will not tell you is that bringing me in makes anything privileged. The honest position, with the four instruction roles and how discovery obligations differ between them, is on /roles. Where a company instructs me directly, the company is the client. Where an individual instructs in a personal matter, the individual is. The letter names one client, and only one.

3.1

Instructions, and who may give them

The letter names the people entitled to instruct me and to authorise an increase in the ceiling. Anything that changes the scope, the ceiling or the deliverable I take in writing only. Ask me by telephone and I will confirm it by email before I act on it, which feels like bureaucracy at five o'clock on a Friday. An unwritten instruction to widen a search is the most common source of a billing dispute in this work.

Material counsel sends me is segregated and logged separately from material I collected myself. That is so the carve-outs at FRCP 26(b)(4)(C) can be honoured without sweeping in protected communications or draft reports, which are protected as trial-preparation material under FRCP 26(b)(4)(B). Those carve-outs are compensation, facts or data provided by counsel and considered, and assumptions provided and relied on.

4

Intake, and what not to send yet

Intake is short on purpose: your name, your role, whether counsel is engaged, a callback number. I do not ask for case detail in a web form. There is no chat widget on this site either, because a chat bubble invites a worried caller to type privileged facts into somebody else's service.

Before the engagement letter is signed, please do not send me:

  • Evidence. No drives, phones or laptops. No exported mailboxes, no images, not one loose file. Anything that arrives before an engagement exists is held sealed and unexamined, or returned unopened. Either way it has been moved for nothing, and the movement is now part of its history.
  • Privileged or confidential documents. Send the question, not the file.
  • Credentials. No passwords, tokens, recovery keys or administrator accounts. At no point in this process will I ask you for a password by email, and an email that does ask is not from me.
  • Regulated data. Protected health information, cardholder data, controlled unclassified information, or personal data under GDPR or UK GDPR. All of it carries handling constraints, and those get settled at scoping, before anything moves.

What does help before signature: the names for the conflicts check, your deadline, the question in one sentence, and a rough inventory of what exists. How many devices, roughly how much data, which platforms, and whether anything is already under a preservation obligation. None of that is evidence, and it is enough for me to scope and price the work.

4.1

Documented authority to examine a device or an account

Before I touch any device, mailbox or account, I confirm in writing who is authorising the examination, on what basis, and for which specific item. That holds whatever the story about ownership. It is the condition clients resist hardest at intake and the one I am least willing to move on.

It applies to a company laptop in a departing employee's possession, a personal phone used for work, a shared household machine, a spouse's device, a former employee's personal mailbox, and a saved session still logged into someone's personal account. "It is our laptop" answers a question about the hardware, not the question about the account.

The reasons are statutory rather than decorative: the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1030; the Stored Communications Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2702, which is why a civil subpoena to a provider does not produce message content; the Wiretap Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2511; and state computer-crime and eavesdropping statutes, which are not uniform. Where authority is unclear I stop and ask. Where it cannot be established I decline that item in writing, rather than examine it and leave the problem to be found in cross-examination.

Suspected child sexual abuse material. I do not take or retain custody of it. If I encounter it, or have reason to think I am about to, I stop, escalate through counsel, and go through law enforcement on access, noting 18 U.S.C. § 3509(m) in federal criminal matters. No engagement letter can agree this away, and you hear it from me before you instruct me, not afterwards. Full procedure on /evidence-handling.
5

Authorisation ceilings

I bill analysis hourly against a ceiling you authorise in writing before work starts. The ceiling is a number attached to a defined phase, and no hour above it is billed. Not as an overage, not at a reduced rate, and not as a courtesy line that appears on the invoice anyway.

I propose the opening authorisation myself, and I show the reasoning: what I expect to look at first, what that should answer, and what would make it take longer. There is no "typical hours" figure anywhere on this site, because a typical-hours figure is a claim about past matters. What you get instead is the opening authorisation I would propose, which you are free to put beside anyone else's.

5.1

The stop-and-report at 80 per cent

At 80% of an authorised ceiling I stop and write to you: hours consumed, what has been established, what is still unexamined, what the remaining work would cost, and whether I think it is worth doing. Work resumes only on a written increase. Silence is not an increase, and neither is a phone call.

That report is also where I tell you the answer is not going to be there, if that is what I have found. Stopping at four-fifths of a ceiling pays me less than running to the end of it. Which is exactly why the stop-point is published here rather than left to my judgement on the day.

5.2

Which class a line falls into

A fixed price buys an artefact of defined extent — an image, an extraction, a copy, a hash set, a certificate. Analysis cannot be priced before someone has looked at the artefact, because the extent of an answer is not knowable in advance. That is why some lines on /rates carry a price and some carry a rate and a ceiling.

Price classes
ClassHow it is authorisedWhat this does not establish
Fixed A stated price for a stated unit, named in the letter. Does not cover analysis of what was acquired, and does not guarantee the artefact answers your question. An image is a copy, not a finding.
Hourly against a ceiling A published rate plus a written stop-point, with the report at 80%. Does not predict the hours. A ceiling caps exposure; it does not tell you what the work will cost.
Scoped after paid triage A fixed, time-boxed first block, credited in full against whatever follows. Does not commit you to the follow-on work, or me to a price for it before the triage output exists.

There is no overage rate and no surge rate. Hours beyond a standby credit bill at the retained rate, not above it.

6

Invoicing

Invoices go out monthly and on completion of a phase. Each one reconciles against the authorisation: hours billed, hours left under the ceiling, deposit balance after the invoice is applied. If an invoice cannot be reconciled against an authorisation you signed, that is my error, and I withdraw it.

Time is recorded in increments of 0.1 hour, one task per entry. There is no block billing. An entry names the date, the timekeeper, the task, the data source it relates to, and the duration. A specimen entry, in the format the invoice uses: 2026-09-14 · principal · keyword search across mailbox export MBX-02 · 1.4. Not "review and analysis, 6.0". The reason is not tidiness. An invoice in this work can end up as an exhibit, and a block-billed entry cannot be defended line by line in a fee application, or under questioning about what I actually did for those six hours.

Expenses go on at cost with no mark-up, with receipts. Travel bills at the published travel rate and is capped per day. Where after-hours or expedited work triggers the uplift, only the out-of-hours hours carry it, and I list those hours separately so you can check the uplift instead of taking it on trust.

6.1

Invoice formats and e-billing portals

As standard you get an itemised PDF invoice, and the same detail again as a CSV time export, one row per entry, which loads into a spreadsheet or a matter-management system without anyone rekeying it.

Electronic-billing portals are a separate question. Portal formats, LEDES among them, impose their own field structure, their own task and activity coding, and their own submission rules.

The invoice format, any task-code set and the submission route are agreed in writing before the first invoice is raised. Where your portal requires a format I cannot produce, I say so at intake and we agree an alternative, or you route it another way. Neither of us discovers this at the first submission.

If a portal is mandated, tell me at intake and send the specification. Sixty days of non-payment because an invoice bounced on a coding fault is avoidable in one conversation before the matter starts.

6.2

Purchase orders and vendor onboarding

An invoice that reaches a corporate accounts-payable system without a purchase order number on it does not get paid. It gets returned, usually silently, and often to nobody in particular. So where a purchase order is required it has to exist before work starts, and its number goes on the engagement letter. If the purchase order carries a value lower than the authorised ceiling, the purchase order value governs and I stop there.

Vendor onboarding typically asks for a vendor form, a W-9, banking details, supplier terms or a master services agreement, security questionnaires, and evidence of insurance at stated limits.

Tell me at intake what your onboarding requires. I answer each requirement individually before a vendor record is opened, including any certificate of insurance at your stated limits, and where one cannot be met you hear that at intake rather than at the first invoice. I do not answer an onboarding questionnaire by attesting to something I have not verified.

My banking details are confirmed by telephone, to a number you already hold, and they do not change mid-matter. An email that appears to come from me changing my payment details is not from me. Call first.

6.3

Payment terms

Invoices are payable within 30 days of issue. Above a threshold stated in the engagement letter I prefer bank transfer, and transfer carries a 3% discount against the card price. That is a payment-method differential, not a discount on the rate schedule, which carries none. Nor is it a card surcharge. A surcharge is a materially different thing, it is unlawful in several states, and it cannot lawfully be applied to a debit card at all.

Pay-when-paid terms are not accepted, in any form. Not on receipt of funds from your client, not on settlement, not at the conclusion of the matter. The reason is the same one that governs the rate: a fee contingent on an outcome, or on somebody else's recovery, turns my compensation into an issue at trial, and the client is the one who pays for that.

7

Retainer mechanics

I take an advance deposit before work starts and apply it against invoices as they issue. The mechanics are printed here in the same words the engagement letter uses, because retainer handling is where a one-person practice most easily says something it is not entitled to say. It is worth being slow and literal about it.

  • The deposit sits in my operating account as an advance deposit against fees. It is not a trust account, not escrow and not IOLTA, and I will not call it any of those here, in a letter, or on an invoice. Those words belong to regulated professions and carry their meanings with them. An examiner who borrows one is describing a protection you do not have.
  • No interest accrues to the payer on a held balance.
  • The unearned balance is refunded in full within 10 business days of matter close, measured from the final invoice. No processing fee and no administration fee. A card-paid deposit comes back at full face value: the processor's cut comes out of my money, not yours.
  • No automatic replenishment. I do not charge a card again without a separately signed authorisation naming the trigger, the amount and a ceiling. A card on file is not permission to top up a balance.
  • A deposit is not an authorisation. Money held authorises no hour of work; only a written ceiling does. A balance larger than the work you authorised does not quietly become permission for more.
8

Closing a matter

A matter closes on a final invoice. The unearned balance comes back on the terms at section 7. Evidence storage runs for 90 days at no charge and then at the published monthly rate until you instruct otherwise. Material is returned or destroyed on written instruction, with a certificate issued where destruction is chosen.

I destroy nothing without a written instruction, and nothing at all while I have notice of a preservation obligation, a litigation hold or a subpoena touching the material. Where compulsory process reaches material I hold, you are told so that you can object, unless the law prohibits me from telling you. The custody, storage and retention terms are on /evidence-handling.

9

The refusals that bind an engagement

The full list is at /#we-will-not and it is repeated verbatim in every engagement letter I sign. Below are the clauses that bear directly on how an instruction is taken and billed, word for word from that list. The numbering there is the numbering to cite.

I will not — extract

  1. Take a fee that depends on what I find, on the outcome of the matter, or on which side retains me. The rate is the same either way and it is set before I look at anything. An examiner paid on the result is impeachable, and the client is the one who pays for that.
  2. Bill an hour above the ceiling you authorised in writing, or run an open-ended password recovery or extraction attempt without a written cap agreed first.
  3. Touch a device, mailbox or account without documented authority from someone entitled to give it, whatever the account holder's relationship to you, and whatever the story about ownership. That includes a spouse's phone, a former employee's personal account, and a saved session on a company laptop.
  4. Take a matter I cannot finish inside your deadline. I will say so on the first call rather than accept it and miss it.
  5. Start work, hold a date, or accept a single file before the conflicts check clears and the engagement letter is signed. A payment made before that point is an offer, held unapplied, and refunded in full.
  6. Carry an overage or surge rate. Hours beyond a standby credit bill at the retained rate, not above it.
  7. Deduct card processing fees from an unearned balance I am returning to you.
  8. Act for both sides in a matter, or act as an appointed neutral where I have advised either side.
  9. Take a matter that requires a certification, a licence or a qualification I do not hold. I identify that at scoping, name it in the engagement letter, and either bring in someone who holds it or decline.
  10. Promise you a finding, a recovered file, an unlocked handset, or that anything I produce will be admitted. Admissibility is the court's decision and nobody can guarantee it.

Where a matter requires a certified examiner, an accredited laboratory, or a licence in the venue, that condition is identified at scoping, stated in the engagement letter, and satisfied before work begins. I check the licensing position for the venue and then either hold the licence, work under one, or decline the matter.

To start

Send the names for the conflicts check, your deadline, and the question in one sentence. Not the evidence. Not the files. On the first call I will tell you whether this is a matter I can take, and if it is not, I will say so on that call rather than sit on it for a week.
