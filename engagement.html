How an instruction starts

Scoping call, conflicts check, engagement letter with rates and a written ceiling, deposit, work. In an incident, the same order compressed into hours; first-hour steps are on /emergency.

No engagement exists until conflicts clear and the letter is signed. Until then I am not retained, I hold no date, and I take custody of nothing. A call, an email or an NDA does not change that. Payment arriving early is an offer: held unapplied, refunded in full, nothing deducted for card processing.

Conflicts

Names first, story second. The check needs full legal names including parents and subsidiaries, opposing counsel, likely custodians, insurers and the venue. Trade names are not enough. An incomplete list gets a partial check, described in writing as partial.

I do not act for both sides, or as a neutral where I have advised either side. Approaches are logged, but a log entry is not a retainer, and a preliminary call stays confidential whether or not you instruct me.

If a conflict surfaces mid-matter I stop where I recognise it, seal custody unexamined, tell you the same day in writing, return or destroy on your instruction, and refund the unearned balance in full within 10 business days. Work already reported is still billed, and your own preservation duty is untouched.

Who the client is, and who may instruct

The letter names one client: whoever signs it. Where counsel instructs for a company, counsel is the client — the report goes to the firm, which decides who else sees it, including the company's IT director. Roles are set out on /roles; instructing me does not by itself make anything privileged.

Scope, ceiling and deliverable changes in writing only. Counsel's material is logged apart from mine, so the carve-outs at FRCP 26(b)(4)(C) are honoured without sweeping in drafts protected under FRCP 26(b)(4)(B).

Intake, and what not to send yet

Intake asks a name, a role, whether counsel is engaged, and a callback number. No case detail in a web form, and no chat widget on this site.

Before signature, do not send evidence — it is held sealed or returned unopened, and has been moved for nothing; privileged documents — send the question, not the file; credentials — I never ask for a password by email; or regulated data, PHI and GDPR included, which is settled at scoping first.

Useful now: the conflicts names, your deadline, the question in one sentence, and a rough inventory — devices, volume, platforms, anything under hold.

Documented authority to examine

Before I touch a device, mailbox or account I confirm in writing who authorises it and for which item: a leaver's laptop, a personal phone used for work, a saved session on a personal account. "It is our laptop" answers a question about hardware, not about the account. Statutes bite here — 18 U.S.C. § 1030, § 2702, § 2511, and state law that is not uniform — so where authority cannot be established I decline that item in writing.

Suspected child sexual abuse material. I take no custody of it: I stop, escalate through counsel, and access goes through law enforcement, noting 18 U.S.C. § 3509(m). No letter can agree this away. Procedure on /evidence-handling.

Ceilings, and the stop at 80 per cent

Analysis bills hourly against a ceiling you authorise in writing before work starts. No hour above it is billed — no overage rate, no surge rate.

At 80% I stop and write: hours used, what is established, what is unexamined, what the rest would cost, and whether it is worth doing. Work resumes on a written increase; silence is not one. That report is also where I tell you the answer is not there.

Fixed prices buy a defined artefact — an image, an extraction, a hash set. Analysis cannot be priced before someone has looked, which is why /rates carries both.

Invoicing

  • Monthly and on completion of a phase, reconciled to the authorisation: hours billed, hours left, deposit balance. One I cannot reconcile to something you signed, I withdraw.
  • Time in 0.1 hour, one task per entry, no block billing: 2026-09-14 · principal · keyword search across mailbox export MBX-02 · 1.4. These end up as exhibits.
  • Itemised PDF and a CSV time export. Portal format and task codes agreed in writing before the first invoice.
  • Expenses at cost, with receipts. Travel at the published travel rate, capped per day; any out-of-hours uplift falls only on out-of-hours hours, listed separately.
  • PO number on the letter before work starts, and where the PO is below the ceiling, the PO governs. Onboarding requirements — W-9, MSA, security questionnaire, insurance limits — at intake, and where one cannot be met you hear it then.
  • Payable within 30 days. Bank transfer carries a 3% discount against the card price: a payment-method differential, not a surcharge. No pay-when-paid terms.

Retainers

  • The deposit sits in my operating account. Not a trust account, not escrow, not IOLTA.
  • No interest accrues to the payer.
  • Unearned balance refunded in full within 10 business days of the final invoice, with no processing or administration fee.
  • No automatic replenishment. A card on file is not permission to top up.
  • A deposit is not an authorisation. Only a written ceiling authorises work.

Closing a matter

A matter closes on a final invoice. Storage runs 90 days free, then at the published monthly rate. Return or destruction on written instruction, with a certificate where destruction is chosen. I destroy nothing touched by a hold or subpoena, and where compulsory process reaches material I hold, you are told so you can object unless the law forbids it. Terms on /evidence-handling.

The refusals that bind an engagement

Repeated verbatim in every letter I sign. Full list at /#we-will-not.

I will not — extract

  1. Take a fee that depends on what I find, on the outcome, or on which side retains me.
  2. Bill an hour above your written ceiling, or run an open-ended password attempt without a cap.
  3. Touch a device or account without documented authority — a spouse's phone, a leaver's personal account, a saved session on a company laptop.
  4. Take a matter I cannot finish inside your deadline.
  5. Start work, hold a date, or accept a file before conflicts clear and the letter is signed.
  6. Carry an overage or surge rate, or deduct processing fees from a balance I am returning.
  7. Act for both sides, or as a neutral where I have advised either side.
  8. Promise a finding, a recovered file, an unlocked handset, or that anything I produce is admitted.

Where a matter requires a certified examiner, an accredited laboratory or a licence in the venue, that is stated in the engagement letter and satisfied before work begins.
