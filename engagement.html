How an instruction starts

Scoping call, conflicts check, engagement letter with rates and a written ceiling, deposit, work. In an incident, the same order compressed into hours — first-hour steps are on /emergency.

There is no engagement until conflicts clear and the letter is signed. Until then I am not retained, I hold no date for you, and I take custody of nothing. A call, an email, an NDA or an introduction from counsel does not change that. Payment arriving early is an offer: held unapplied, refunded in full, nothing deducted for card processing.

Conflicts

Names first, story second. To run the check I need full legal names including parents and subsidiaries, opposing counsel, likely custodians, insurers, and the venue. Trade names are not enough. An incomplete list gets a partial check, described in writing as partial.

I do not act for both sides, or as an appointed neutral where I have advised either side. Every approach is logged — date, parties, venue, subject, outcome. A log entry is not a retainer and not a disqualification, and a preliminary call stays confidential whether or not you instruct me.

Who the client is, and who may instruct

The letter names one client: whoever signs it. Where counsel instructs for a company, counsel is the client — the report goes to the firm, and the firm decides who else sees it, including the company's IT director. The four roles are on /roles; instructing me does not by itself make anything privileged.

Scope, ceiling and deliverable changes in writing only. Ask by phone and I confirm by email before acting. Counsel's material is logged separately from mine, so the carve-outs at FRCP 26(b)(4)(C) are honoured without sweeping in drafts protected under FRCP 26(b)(4)(B).

Intake, and what not to send yet

Intake asks a name, a role, whether counsel is engaged, a callback number. No case detail in a web form, and no chat widget on this site.

Before signature, do not send: evidence — it is held sealed or returned unopened, and has been moved for nothing; privileged documents — send the question, not the file; credentials — I never ask for a password by email, and an email that does is not from me; regulated data — PHI, cardholder, CUI, GDPR, all settled at scoping first.

Useful now: the conflicts names, your deadline, the question in one sentence, and a rough inventory — devices, volume, platforms, anything already under hold.

Documented authority to examine

Before I touch a device, mailbox or account I confirm in writing who authorises it and for which item — a leaver's company laptop, a personal phone used for work, a former employee's mailbox, a saved session on a personal account. "It is our laptop" answers a question about hardware, not about the account. The reasons are statutory: 18 U.S.C. § 1030, § 2702 (why a civil subpoena to a provider yields no content), § 2511, and state statutes that are not uniform. Where authority cannot be established I decline that item in writing.

Suspected child sexual abuse material. I take no custody of it. I stop, escalate through counsel, and access goes through law enforcement, noting 18 U.S.C. § 3509(m). No engagement letter can agree this away. Procedure on /evidence-handling.

Ceilings, and the stop at 80 per cent

Analysis bills hourly against a ceiling you authorise in writing before work starts. No hour above it is billed — no overage rate, no surge rate.

At 80% I stop and write: hours used, what is established, what is unexamined, what the rest would cost, and whether it is worth doing. Work resumes on a written increase; silence is not one. That report is also where I tell you the answer is not there, which pays me less than running to the ceiling.

Fixed prices buy an artefact of defined extent — an image, an extraction, a hash set. Analysis cannot be priced before someone has looked, which is why some lines on /rates carry a price and others a rate and a ceiling.

Invoicing

  • Monthly and on completion of a phase, each reconciled to the authorisation: hours billed, hours left, deposit balance. If it cannot be reconciled to something you signed, I withdraw it.
  • Time in 0.1 hour, one task per entry, no block billing: 2026-09-14 · principal · keyword search across mailbox export MBX-02 · 1.4. An invoice here can end up as an exhibit.
  • Itemised PDF and a CSV time export. Portal format and task codes agreed in writing before the first invoice.
  • Expenses at cost, with receipts. Travel at the published travel rate, capped per day. Any out-of-hours uplift falls only on out-of-hours hours, listed separately.
  • Purchase order number on the letter before work starts. Where the PO is below the ceiling, the PO governs. Send onboarding requirements at intake — W-9, MSA, security questionnaire, insurance limits — and where one cannot be met you hear it then.
  • Payable within 30 days. Bank transfer carries a 3% discount against the card price: a payment-method differential, not a surcharge.
  • No pay-when-paid terms, in any form. Banking details are confirmed by phone to a number you already hold and never change mid-matter.

Retainers

  • The deposit sits in my operating account. Not a trust account, not escrow, not IOLTA.
  • No interest accrues to the payer.
  • Unearned balance refunded in full within 10 business days of the final invoice. No processing or administration fee; a card-paid deposit comes back at face value.
  • No automatic replenishment. A card on file is not permission to top up.
  • A deposit is not an authorisation. Only a written ceiling authorises work.

Closing a matter

A matter closes on a final invoice. Storage runs 90 days free, then at the published monthly rate. Return or destruction on written instruction, certificate where destruction is chosen. I destroy nothing touched by a hold or subpoena, and where compulsory process reaches material I hold you are told so you can object, unless the law forbids it. Terms on /evidence-handling.

The refusals that bind an engagement

Repeated verbatim in every letter I sign. Full list at /#we-will-not.

I will not — extract

  1. Take a fee that depends on what I find, on the outcome, or on which side retains me.
  2. Bill an hour above your written ceiling, or run an open-ended password attempt without a cap.
  3. Touch a device, mailbox or account without documented authority — a spouse's phone, a leaver's personal account, a saved session on a company laptop.
  4. Take a matter I cannot finish inside your deadline.
  5. Start work, hold a date, or accept a file before conflicts clear and the letter is signed.
  6. Carry an overage or surge rate, or deduct processing fees from a balance I am returning.
  7. Act for both sides, or act as a neutral where I have advised either side.
  8. Take a matter needing a certification or licence I do not hold.
  9. Promise a finding, a recovered file, an unlocked handset, or that anything I produce is admitted.

Where a matter requires a certified examiner, an accredited laboratory or a licence in the venue, that is identified at scoping, stated in the engagement letter, and satisfied before work begins.
