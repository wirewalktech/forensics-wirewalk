What a standby agreement is

A one-year agreement, signed while nothing is happening, that settles everything which has to be settled before I can touch a single device. You give me a list of entities and I run the conflicts check against it in advance. The engagement terms and the rate schedule are agreed and held for the term. Each side names a contact. A short-form authorisation to begin out-of-hours work is signed early, so that the first hour of an incident is not spent hunting for somebody with the authority to sign one. And the agreement carries a stated response commitment, written into a signed document, which is the only place one exists.

The argument for it is narrow and I would rather put it without decoration. In an incident the forensic work is rarely what is slow. What is slow is procurement, conflicts and signature: a vendor form that wants a security questionnaire returned first, a conflicts check that has to run before anyone may look at a single file, an authorisation ceiling that needs a signatory who is asleep. Between them they can cost you a day. What the day costs is log retention that rolls, volatile memory that goes the moment somebody reboots, a backup cycle that expires on its own schedule. And the laptop, which by then has been helpfully reimaged. None of that comes back afterwards, at any price.

1.1

What it is not

It is not insurance and it is not a substitute for a cyber policy. /insurance before you read the rest of this page; if your policy mandates a panel, most of what follows is academic.

It is not a reservation of capacity either. I cannot sell the same night twice, and a firm that tells you otherwise is selling you a place in a queue it has not shown you. Nor is it a block of prepaid hours in the ordinary sense, because the fee is credited against the work rather than drawn down as a balance. And it is not a promise that I will be the right person for whatever happens. Some incidents belong with a panel consultancy, and I will say so.

2.1

How the conflicts list is handled

The list is confidential and I use it for nothing else. If a name does not clear, I will tell you that the name is not clear and then tell you nothing further. Not who, not when, not in what capacity. The existence of a conflict is itself confidential information belonging to somebody else. What you can do with that is instruct someone else against the name, knowing in advance rather than on the day.

If a conflict surfaces mid-matter, the protocol is written down before it is needed, which is the only reason it survives contact with pressure. Work stops. The evidence is sequestered unexamined. The retaining party is told the same day, material is returned or destroyed on instruction, and the unearned balance is refunded in full. Nothing learned is disclosed to anyone, including to the party the conflict runs to. Preliminary approaches are logged for conflicts purposes even where they go nowhere; the logging creates no engagement, and what was said in them stays confidential.

For its term, the agreement records that I will not accept an instruction adverse to you in any matter arising from the systems, people or entities it names.

3

The fee, the credit and the retained rate

The annual fee is $7,500, credited in full against the first engagement of the year. It is the figure published on the rate schedule and it is the figure in the agreement. It is not quoted case by case.

Credited in full means what it sounds like: the fee is applied against the first invoice of the year at face value, not converted into some smaller number of hours at a higher rate. The retained rate is the published schedule at /rates less 15%, applied to hourly lines and fixed prices alike, with the out-of-hours and expedited uplift waived for the first 48 hours of an incident. Expenses, media and platform licences are still passed through at cost with no mark-up, which is also why no discount applies to them. There is no margin in a pass-through to discount.

3.2

There is no surge rate and no overage rate

This is the paragraph worth reading twice, because the surge clause is where cheap retainers turn expensive elsewhere in this market. Hours beyond a standby credit bill at the retained rate, not above it. No overage multiplier. No incident-rate schedule that quietly supersedes the retained one, no premium band that engages once the credit is exhausted, and no uplift triggered by the fact that a matter is urgent. Urgency is the thing the agreement was bought for. The only uplift that exists anywhere on my schedule is the out-of-hours and expedited one: it is mechanically triggered, it applies to the out-of-hours hours rather than to all time on the matter, and under a standby agreement it is waived for the first 48 hours of an incident.

A test you can put to any standby or retainer offer, mine included. Ask what an hour costs on the worst night of the year, then ask what it costs on an ordinary Tuesday. If the two numbers differ, the cheap annual fee is a deposit against a rate you have not been shown.

3.3

If the year passes and nothing happens

The fee does not expire. At the annual review you can convert an unused standby into a tabletop exercise and plan review, published at $6,500, or a cloud logging and evidence-readiness review for one tenant, published at $4,200. both indicative; confirmed at scoping Either of those is something you would otherwise be buying separately. The conversion is priced at the retained rate, like everything else under the agreement, so at the published figures it comes to $5,525 or $3,570. Both sit below the annual fee, and the difference carries into the following term. If you and I agree instead on something that sits above the fee, the balance is invoiced at the retained rate before the work starts. Nothing is forfeited for not having had an incident, which seems to me the right way round.

4

Who should not buy one

A page that only argues for the purchase is a sales page. So here is the other side of it, written against my own interest. A standby agreement sold to an organisation that will never use it is a bad transaction, and it is a bad transaction I have to renew in front of you every year.

5

What the agreement does not buy

Under a standby agreement I will not

  1. Carry an overage or surge rate. Hours beyond the credit bill at the retained rate, not above it, whatever the hour of the night and whatever the matter turns out to be.
  2. Guarantee that I can take the matter. A conflict can arise during the term, and one examiner can be committed elsewhere. The commitment is to respond within the stated interval and to tell you immediately, so that what you lose is minutes rather than a day.
  3. Vary the rate according to what I find, the outcome of the matter, or which side of it you are on. The standby discount is fixed at signature and it does not move with the work.
  4. Bill an hour above the ceiling you authorised in writing, including under the pre-signed out-of-hours authorisation, which names its own ceiling.
  5. Take a card-on-file authorisation to replenish anything automatically. Any recurring authority needs a separately signed instrument naming the trigger, the amount and a ceiling.
  6. Deduct card processing fees from an unearned balance I am returning to you, on termination, on a conflict, or on a renewal you decline.
  7. Renew the agreement without your signature, or let it renew quietly. A standby that lapses into a subscription is not a considered purchase.
  8. Agree a blanket conflicts block across an industry, across a corporate group nobody has told me about, or against an unnamed class of adversary. Clearance runs against the list you give me, and the list is what I can honour.
  9. Promise a finding, a recovered file, an unlocked handset, or that anything I produce will be admitted. Admissibility is the court's decision and nobody can guarantee it.
  10. Take a matter that requires a certification, a licence or a qualification I do not hold. That is identified at scoping, named in the engagement letter, and then either met by bringing in someone who holds it, or the matter is declined. I check the licensing position for your venue and either hold it, work under one, or decline
6

How the year runs

At signature: the conflicts list is supplied and run, the terms and any vendor onboarding are completed, contacts are named on both sides, and the out-of-hours authorisation and its opening ceiling are agreed. I ask for one further thing, which costs nothing and is worth more than most of the rest of it. A short note of where your logs live, what retention they carry, and who can authorise access to each system out of hours. Establishing exactly that is ordinarily how the first hour of an incident gets spent. It can be settled instead on a quiet afternoon, a year earlier, for nothing.

During the term, you tell me when the conflicts list changes and I re-run it. If your counsel changes, if you acquire an entity, or if a dispute starts to look foreseeable with a party who is not on the list, that is the moment to send me the name. Not the week you need me.

The annual review is a call, included in the fee. It covers what changed, whether the response commitment is still the right one, the rate schedule for the following term, and what you want to do with an unused credit. Rates are held for twelve months from the date of the agreement and change only on 60 days' written notice; work already authorised bills at the rate authorised.

6.1

Termination and what happens to the money

Either party may terminate on written notice. Where the agreement ends before the credit has been used, the unearned balance is refunded in full, without a processing deduction, within ten business days. Where I terminate because a conflict has arisen, the same applies, and the refund is not conditional on anything. One point to be blunt about: an advance held under the agreement sits in the operating account. It is not trust, it is not escrow, it is not a client account, and it earns no interest for the payer. I would rather say that in the flattest words available than let anyone assume otherwise.

7

Getting one in place

The first step is a scoping call and it is free. Where the answer is that you do not need this agreement, that is what I will say on the call, and the call is free on exactly the same terms. What I want to know is what a bad day looks like for you, what obligations attach to it, who can sign at midnight, and whether your insurance position makes a non-panel examiner usable at all. If the answer to that last one is no, I will tell you so, and the conversation is over in ten minutes.
