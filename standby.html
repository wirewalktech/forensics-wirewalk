1

What a standby agreement is

It is a one-year agreement, signed while nothing is happening, that settles everything which has to be settled before a forensic examiner can touch anything. The conflicts check is run in advance against a list of entities you name. The engagement terms and the rate schedule are agreed and held for the term. A contact is named on both sides. A short-form authorisation to begin out-of-hours work is signed in advance, so the first hour of an incident is not spent finding a signatory. And the agreement carries a stated response commitment: an interval written into a signed document, which is the only place one exists.

The argument for it is narrow and it is worth stating without decoration. In an incident, the forensic work is rarely what is slow. What is slow is procurement, conflicts and signature: a vendor form that needs a security questionnaire returned, a conflicts check that has to be run before a single file can be looked at, an authorisation ceiling that needs someone with spending authority who is asleep. Those three things can cost a day between them, and the day costs log retention, volatile memory, a backup expiry cycle and a reimaged laptop. None of that day is recoverable afterwards at any price.

1.1

What it is not

It is not insurance, and it is not a substitute for a cyber policy — see /insurance, and read it before you read the rest of this page. It is not a reservation of capacity: a practice of this size cannot sell the same night twice, and any firm that tells you otherwise is selling you a place in a queue it has not shown you. It is not a block of prepaid hours in the ordinary sense, because the fee is credited against the work rather than drawn down as a balance. And it is not a promise that we will be the right firm for whatever happens. Some incidents belong with a panel consultancy and we will say so.

2.1

How the conflicts list is handled

The list is confidential and is used for nothing else. If a name on it does not clear, we tell you that the name is not clear and we tell you nothing further — not who, not when, not in what capacity — because the existence of a conflict is itself confidential information belonging to somebody else. You are then free to instruct someone else against that name, knowing in advance rather than on the day.

If a conflict surfaces mid-matter, the protocol is written down before it is needed and it does not change under pressure: work stops, the evidence is sequestered unexamined, the retaining party is told the same day, material is returned or destroyed on instruction, the unearned balance is refunded in full, and nothing learned is disclosed to anyone — including to the party the conflict runs to. Preliminary approaches are logged for conflicts purposes even where they go nowhere. The logging creates no engagement and what was said stays confidential.

For its term, the agreement records that we will not accept an instruction adverse to you in any matter arising from the systems, people or entities the agreement names.

3

The fee, the credit and the retained rate

The annual fee is $7,500, credited in full against the first engagement of the year. It is the figure published on the rate schedule and it is the figure in the agreement; it is not quoted case by case.

Credited in full means the fee is applied against the first invoice of the year at its face value, not converted into a smaller number of hours at a higher rate. The retained rate is the published schedule at /rates less 15%, applied to hourly lines and fixed prices alike, with the out-of-hours and expedited uplift waived for the first 48 hours of an incident. Expenses, media, and platform licences continue to be passed through at cost with no mark-up, which is also why no discount applies to them: there is no margin in a pass-through to discount.

3.2

There is no surge rate and no overage rate

This is the line worth reading twice, because it is where cheap retainers become expensive elsewhere in this market. Hours beyond a standby credit bill at the retained rate, not above it. There is no overage multiplier, no incident-rate schedule that supersedes the retained one, no premium band that engages once the credit is exhausted, and no uplift triggered by the fact that a matter is urgent — urgency is what the agreement was bought for. The only uplift that exists anywhere on our schedule is the out-of-hours and expedited uplift, it is mechanically triggered, it applies only to the out-of-hours hours rather than to all time on the matter, and under a standby agreement it is waived for the first 48 hours of an incident.

The practical test to apply to any standby or retainer offer, ours included: ask what an hour costs on the worst night of the year, and compare it to what an hour costs on an ordinary Tuesday. If the two numbers differ, the cheap annual fee is a deposit against a rate you have not seen.

3.3

If the year passes and nothing happens

The fee does not expire. An unused standby converts, at your election at the annual review, into a tabletop exercise and plan review, published at $6,500, or a cloud logging and evidence-readiness review for one tenant, published at $4,200 — either of which you would otherwise be buying separately. both indicative; confirmed at scoping The conversion is priced at the retained rate, like everything else under the agreement, so at those published figures it is $5,525 or $3,570. Both sit below the annual fee, so the difference carries into the following term. Where an election agreed instead of those two sits above the fee, the balance is invoiced at the retained rate before the work starts. Nothing is forfeited for not having had an incident, which we regard as the correct outcome to reward.

5

What the agreement does not buy

Under a standby agreement we will not

  1. Carry an overage or surge rate. Hours beyond the credit bill at the retained rate, not above it, whatever the hour of the night and whatever the matter turns out to be.
  2. Guarantee that we can take the matter. A conflict can arise during the term, and a practice of this size can be committed elsewhere. The commitment is to respond within the stated interval and to tell you immediately, so you lose minutes rather than a day.
  3. Vary the rate according to what we find, the outcome of the matter, or which side of it you are on. The standby discount is fixed at signature and it does not move with the work.
  4. Bill an hour above the ceiling you authorised in writing, including under the pre-signed out-of-hours authorisation, which names its own ceiling.
  5. Take a card-on-file authorisation to replenish anything automatically. Any recurring authority requires a separately signed instrument naming the trigger, the amount and a ceiling.
  6. Deduct card processing fees from an unearned balance we are returning to you, on termination, on a conflict, or on renewal declined.
  7. Renew the agreement without your signature, or let it renew silently. A standby that lapses into a subscription is not a considered purchase.
  8. Agree a blanket conflicts block across an industry, a corporate group we have not been told about, or an unnamed class of adversary. Clearance runs against the list you give us and the list is what we can honour.
  9. Promise a finding, a recovered file, an unlocked handset, or that anything we produce will be admitted. Admissibility is the court's decision and no one can guarantee it.
  10. Take a matter that requires a certification, a licence or a qualification we do not hold. That is identified at scoping, named in the engagement letter, and either met by bringing in someone who holds it or the matter is declined. we check the licensing position for your venue and either hold it, work under one, or decline
6

How the year runs

At signature: the conflicts list is supplied and run, the terms and any vendor onboarding are completed, contacts are named on both sides, and the out-of-hours authorisation and its opening ceiling are agreed. We ask for one further thing that costs nothing and is worth more than the rest of it — a short note of where your logs live, what retention they carry, and who can authorise access to each system out of hours. Establishing exactly that is ordinarily part of the first hour of an incident, and it is the part that can be settled in advance on a quiet afternoon at no cost.

During the term: you tell us when the conflicts list changes, and we re-run it. If your counsel changes, if you acquire an entity, or if a dispute becomes foreseeable with a party not on the list, that is the moment to send a name rather than the week you need us.

At the annual review: a call, included in the fee, covering what changed, whether the response commitment is still the right one, the rate schedule for the following term, and the election on an unused credit. Rates are held for twelve months from the date of the agreement and change only on 60 days' written notice; work already authorised bills at the rate authorised.

6.1

Termination and what happens to the money

Either party may terminate on written notice. Where the agreement ends before the credit has been used, the unearned balance is refunded in full, without a processing deduction, within ten business days. Where we terminate because a conflict has arisen, the same applies and the refund is not conditional on anything. An advance held under the agreement sits in the operating account, is not described as trust, escrow or a client account, and earns no interest for the payer — we would rather say that in the plainest words available than let anyone assume otherwise.

7

Getting one in place

The first step is a scoping call, which is free. Where the answer is that you do not need this agreement, that is what we will say on the call, and the call is free on exactly the same terms. What we will want to know is what a bad day looks like for you, what obligations attach to it, who can sign at midnight, and whether your insurance position makes a non-panel examiner usable at all. If the answer to the last question is no, we will tell you on that call and the conversation is over in ten minutes.
