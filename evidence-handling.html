These are the terms in the engagement letter, and they govern from signature. If your own policy needs something else — shorter retention, your own key, media that never leaves your building — say so at scoping. It gets written in, or I decline the matter.

What it costs

Storage after the matter closes — $75 per matter per month up to 4 TB, $15/TB/month above that. The first 90 days after the final invoice are included.

Certified destruction — $150 per matter, certificate included.

A subpoena served on me for your material — published hourly rate, written ceiling, charged to the party whose material it is.

What you end up holding

A written chain of custody, delivered with the report, kept as the work happens rather than reassembled afterwards. Analysis runs on a working copy; nobody works from the master image.

Custody rules

  • Authority first. Nothing enters custody until the authority to access it is written down. Where it is contested — a BYOD handset, a spouse's offered password — I stop and ask counsel. Unauthorised access reaches the examiner too (18 U.S.C. § 1030).
  • At rest. Encrypted, access-controlled, separate from other matters. I hold the keys. No third-party cloud without your written consent naming provider and region.
  • Access. Only the people named in the letter, every access logged. Release requests are confirmed with a known contact on a different channel.
  • Withdrawal. Re-hashed every time it leaves the store. On a mismatch nothing is used or produced: work stops and you hear before I know why, not after.
  • Subcontracting. None by default. If a matter needs a capability I do not hold, I name the provider and get your consent in writing first, never afterwards.

Extension on written instruction at the storage rate. Under a litigation hold I go on holding and say so. Nothing is destroyed without written instruction naming the items; afterwards I issue a signed certificate of items, digests, method and date, asked for or not.

On a subpoena served on me, where notice is lawful I tell you at once and treat the compliance date as my deadline. Whether to object or move to quash is counsel's call (Fed. R. Civ. P. 45(d)(2)(B) allows fourteen days); I preserve, I notify, I hand you the window.

Suspected child sexual abuse material — work stops immediately

This rule is absolute. An engagement letter cannot vary it, and neither can an instruction, a protective order or a client's preference.

If material that appears to be child sexual abuse material is encountered at any point — in triage, during an acquisition, during examination, in a carved artefact, in a thumbnail cache — examination stops at that moment. The material is not opened further, not copied, not extracted, not exported into a report, not attached to an email, and not shown to the client. Any image or container already in my custody is isolated and secured in place. I then contact the retaining party's counsel and, on counsel's instruction and without avoidable delay, law enforcement.

This is not squeamishness and it is not house policy. Knowing possession is itself an offence under 18 U.S.C. § 2252 and § 2252A, and there is no forensic exception for a private examiner. Copying it to preserve it. Viewing more of it to be sure. Carrying it to another facility to look at it properly. Each of those is an independently unlawful act.

In federal criminal matters, 18 U.S.C. § 3509(m) keeps such material with the Government or the court, inspected at a Government facility. The route runs through counsel and the court, not a copy in my store.

Clients hear this before they hire me. If the rule engages mid-matter, work stops and the engagement may end there.
