These are contractual terms, not a facility tour. Everything below is set out in the engagement letter and governs from the moment it is signed. This page is published so that you can read the handling terms before you instruct anyone, and so that the version you are quoted later is the version you were shown — the document identifier and revision date at the head of this page are the ones that appear in the letter. Where your own policy requires a handling arrangement different from the one below — a named jurisdiction, a customer-managed key, media that never leaves your premises, a shorter retention period — that is settled at scoping and written in, or we decline the matter. Terms in force from signature. No material is accepted before that point.
1

Authority before custody

Nothing is collected, copied, opened or accepted into custody until authority to access it has been confirmed and written down. The record names the person who gave the authority, the capacity in which they gave it, the devices and accounts it covers, and the date. Where authority is unclear, contested, or given by someone whose entitlement to give it we cannot establish, we stop and put the question to counsel rather than resolve it ourselves.

1.1

Why this is a handling term and not a formality

Access without authority is not merely a civil problem for the client. The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1030, the Wiretap Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2511, and state computer-crime and eavesdropping statutes reach the examiner as readily as anyone else. The Stored Communications Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2702, is the reason a civil subpoena to a provider does not produce message content, and the reason a collection has to be built on the account holder's own authority or a court's order rather than on a convenient credential.

The flashpoints are predictable, which is why the rule is written this way: a handset enrolled under a BYOD policy nobody has read since it was written; a household machine used by two people now adverse to each other; a former employee's personal mailbox reachable from a saved session on a company laptop; credentials handed over by a spouse or a co-owner. In each we ask who is entitled to give access to this account or this device, and we decline until the answer is documented.

2

Custody documentation

A written chain of custody is opened the moment anything is received, imaged or exported, and it is written as the work happens rather than assembled afterwards from memory. It follows the item until it is returned or destroyed, and it is produced with the report without being asked for.

3

Storage and encryption at rest

Evidence held for a matter is kept in an access-controlled store, encrypted at rest, physically and logically separated from general business data and from other matters. Transport media are encrypted before they leave the premises. Keys are held by the principal and are not stored with the media they protect. Where an engagement requires a specific cryptographic standard, a validated module, or a named storage jurisdiction, that condition is stated in the engagement letter and satisfied before work begins.

Evidence is not placed in a third-party cloud service, a shared file-transfer service or a consumer account without your written consent naming the provider. Where volume or format makes a hosted platform the only practical option, we name the platform and the region and obtain that consent before anything is uploaded.

Storage after the matter closes is charged at $75 per matter per month up to 4 TB, $15/TB/month above that. The first 90 days after the final invoice are included.

3.1

Access authority and logging

Access to a matter's evidence is limited to the people named in the engagement letter for that matter. Every access is logged: the person, the date and time, the copy touched, and the reason. The log is part of the custody record and is produced with it. It is kept for the same period as the material.

Nobody outside that list gets access without your written instruction — including your colleagues, your client's IT staff, your insurer, and anyone presenting themselves by telephone as acting for you. Release requests are confirmed against a known contact by a route other than the one the request arrived on.

3.2

Withdrawal and re-verification

Material is re-verified by hash whenever it comes out of store: before analysis, before production, and on return or transfer to anyone. The digest computed on withdrawal is recorded against the digest computed at acquisition, and both appear in the custody record.

If a digest does not match, the material is not used and not produced. The mismatch is recorded, work stops, and you are told at the first opportunity — before we have worked out what caused it, not after. Where your matter requires a fixed notification window, that window is stated in the engagement letter; we publish no response commitment outside a signed standby agreement. A re-verification failure that surfaces late in a matter is a far worse outcome for you than one reported when it happens, and the temptation to investigate quietly first is exactly the thing this term exists to remove.

4

Retention after the matter closes

The default is a fixed period, stated in the engagement letter, running from the final invoice. Unless you instruct otherwise in writing, the default period is twelve months.

4.1

Destruction and the certificate

Nothing is destroyed without a written instruction from the retaining party, and the instruction must identify the matter and the items. On completion we issue a certificate of destruction recording the items, the identifiers and digests by which they were held, the method used, the date and time, and the person who carried it out. The certificate is signed and is issued whether or not anyone asks for it.

The certificate covers the copies we held. It is not, and does not purport to be, a statement about copies held by you, by the opposing party, by a platform provider or by anyone else.

Certified destruction $150 per matter. The certificate is not charged separately.

5

Subpoenas and other legal process for material we hold

Material held for your matter can attract process — a subpoena to us as a non-party, a preservation letter, a regulator's demand, a search warrant. The handling is fixed in advance so that it is not improvised under a deadline.

5.1

What happens on receipt

We notify the retaining party as soon as we are able, and we treat the compliance date on the process as our own deadline for doing it, wherever notice is lawful — which it is in the ordinary civil case and is not where a court has sealed the process or a statute forbids disclosure. Where notice is prohibited, we say so as soon as we are permitted to say anything at all. We do not volunteer material, we do not comply ahead of the date for compliance, and we do not treat a subpoena addressed to us as a licence to discuss the matter.

A subpoena to a non-party to produce documents or electronically stored information may be met with a written objection under Fed. R. Civ. P. 45(d)(2)(B), served before the earlier of the time specified for compliance or fourteen days after the subpoena is served. That window is short. The objection is served by us as the recipient, but whether one is served is your decision and your counsel's; our part is to get the document to you inside the window, to identify what we hold that is within its terms, and to preserve everything in scope from the moment the process arrives. Whether to object, to move to quash or to modify, and on what grounds — including privilege, work product and the protections for draft reports and attorney–expert communications under Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(4)(B) and 26(b)(4)(C) — is counsel's decision and we do not make it for you. Where a court orders production over your objection, we comply with the order and tell you what was produced.

We charge time spent responding to third-party process at the published hourly rate, to the party whose material it is, with the usual written ceiling. That is stated here rather than discovered on an invoice.

6

Subcontracting

The default is that nothing is subcontracted. Where a matter needs capability we do not hold — a specialist extraction, a laboratory process, a hosted review platform, a certified or licensed examiner for your venue — we name the intended provider, say what they would receive and what they would do with it, and obtain your written consent before anything leaves our custody. Consent is sought in advance, never ratified afterwards, and a refusal is a legitimate answer that may mean we decline the matter.

Anyone engaged under this clause is bound to these handling terms, is entered in the custody record as a named custodian, and is subject to the same conflicts check as we are. Material does not leave the jurisdiction without your written consent naming the destination.

7

Suspected child sexual abuse material — work stops immediately

This rule is absolute and it cannot be varied by an engagement letter, an instruction, a protective order or a client's preference.

If material that appears to be child sexual abuse material is encountered at any point — during triage, during acquisition, during examination, in a carved artefact, in a thumbnail cache — examination stops at that moment. The material is not opened further, not copied, not extracted, not exported into a report, not attached to an email, and not shown to the client. Any image or container already in our custody is isolated and secured in place. We then contact the retaining party's counsel and, on counsel's instruction and without avoidable delay, law enforcement.

The reason is not squeamishness and it is not policy. Knowing possession is itself an offence under 18 U.S.C. § 2252 and § 2252A, and there is no forensic exception for a private examiner. Copying it to preserve it, viewing more of it to be sure, or carrying it to another facility to look properly are each independently unlawful acts, and an examiner who does them has committed an offence while trying to be diligent.

In federal criminal matters, 18 U.S.C. § 3509(m) keeps such material in the care, custody and control of the Government or the court, and directs the court to deny a defence request to copy or otherwise reproduce it for so long as the Government makes it reasonably available — which the section defines as ample opportunity for inspection, viewing and examination at a Government facility, by the defendant, defence counsel, and anyone the defendant seeks to qualify to give expert testimony. That is the route: access through counsel and the court, at a Government facility, on the court's terms — not a copy in our store.

Clients are told this before they hire us rather than after. If the rule engages mid-matter, work stops, the balance of any authorisation is unspent, and the engagement may end there.

8

Regulated and restricted data

Engagements touching protected health information, financial data under the Gramm–Leach–Bliley Act, personal data under the GDPR or UK GDPR, controlled unclassified information, or data subject to a protective order carry handling constraints of their own. Those are identified at scoping and written into the engagement letter — including any business associate agreement, data processing terms, transfer mechanism, or restriction on where the data may be processed. They are settled before collection, because a constraint discovered after an image exists is a constraint that has already been breached.

9

What these terms do not do

This page describes handling. It is worth saying plainly what handling does not buy, because a custody record is routinely offered as though it settled questions it does not touch.

The handling commitments and their limits
Commitment What this does not establish
Documented chain of custody from receipt to disposal That the evidence is admissible, or that its authenticity will not be contested. Authentication is built at collection time and decided by the court; a custody record supports the foundation, it does not supply the ruling

Encryption at rest, access control, and a logged access list That the store is accredited or inspected, or that we hold any certification, licence or laboratory accreditation. Where an engagement requires one, it is named in the engagement letter and met before work begins, or the matter is declined

Where these terms sit

The handling terms above are attached to the engagement letter and are read with the method, the rate schedule and the refusals. If a term on this page and a term in a signed engagement letter differ, the engagement letter governs — and the difference will have been negotiated in the open rather than arrived at by silence.
