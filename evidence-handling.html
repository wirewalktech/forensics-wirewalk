These are contractual terms, not a facility tour. Everything below is set out in the engagement letter and governs from the moment it is signed. I publish it here so you can read how your material will be handled before you instruct anybody, and so that the version quoted to you later is the version you were shown. The document identifier and revision date at the head of this page are the ones that appear in the letter. If your own policy requires something these terms do not give you, a shorter retention period or a customer-managed key or media that never leaves your building, raise it at scoping. It gets written in, or I decline the matter. Terms in force from signature. No material is accepted before that point.
1

Authority before custody

I do not collect, copy, open or accept anything into custody until the authority to access it has been confirmed and written down. The record names the person who gave that authority, the capacity they gave it in, the devices and accounts it covers, and the date. Where authority is unclear, or contested, or given by someone whose entitlement to give it I cannot establish, I stop and put the question to counsel rather than answer it myself.

1.1

Why this is a handling term and not a formality

Access without authority is not only the client's problem. The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1030, the Wiretap Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2511, and state computer-crime and eavesdropping statutes reach the examiner as readily as they reach anyone else. The Stored Communications Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2702, is why a civil subpoena to a provider will not produce message content, and why a collection has to rest on the account holder's own authority or a court's order rather than on a convenient credential.

The situations that cause the trouble are always the same few. A handset enrolled under a BYOD policy nobody has read since it was written. A household machine used by two people who are now adverse to each other. A former employee's personal mailbox, still reachable from a saved session on a company laptop. Credentials handed over by a spouse, helpfully, before anyone has asked whose account it actually is. In each of them the question is the same: who is entitled to give access to this account or this device. I decline until the answer is documented. It is a slow answer to give somebody who wants to start this afternoon, and it is the one I am least willing to move on.

2

Custody documentation

A written chain of custody opens the moment anything is received, imaged or exported, and it is written while the work happens rather than reassembled afterwards out of memory. It follows the item until the item is returned or destroyed. You get it with the report, without having to ask.

Analysis is done on a working copy. The master image sits separately, under access control, with its own integrity record, and nobody works from it.

3

Storage and encryption at rest

Evidence held for a matter is kept in an access-controlled store, encrypted at rest, physically and logically separated from general business data and from other matters. Transport media are encrypted before they leave the building. I hold the keys, and they are not stored with the media they protect. Where an engagement requires a specific cryptographic standard, a validated module or a named storage jurisdiction, that condition is stated in the engagement letter and satisfied before work begins.

Evidence does not go into a third-party cloud service, a shared file-transfer service or a consumer account without your written consent naming the provider. Volume or format sometimes makes a hosted platform the only practical way to do the work. When it does, I name the platform and the region and get that consent before anything is uploaded.

Storage after the matter closes is charged at $75 per matter per month up to 4 TB, $15/TB/month above that. The first 90 days after the final invoice are included.

3.1

Access authority and logging

Access to a matter's evidence is limited to the people named in the engagement letter for that matter. Every access is logged: who, when, which copy, and why. The log is part of the custody record, is produced with it, and is kept for as long as the material is.

Nobody outside that list gets access on anything but your written instruction. That includes your colleagues, your client's IT staff, your insurer, and anyone who telephones saying they act for you. I confirm release requests with a known contact, by a route other than the one the request arrived on.

3.2

Withdrawal and re-verification

Material is re-verified by hash whenever it comes out of store: before analysis, before production, and on return or transfer to anyone. The digest computed on withdrawal is recorded against the digest computed at acquisition, and both appear in the custody record.

If a digest does not match, the material is not used and not produced. The mismatch is recorded, work stops, and you are told at the first opportunity, before I have worked out what caused it rather than after. Where your matter requires a fixed notification window, that window is stated in the engagement letter; I publish no response commitment outside a signed standby agreement. I would far rather make that call early and look foolish than hand you a verification failure in the week before a hearing. The temptation to have a quiet look first is the whole reason this term is written down.

4

Retention after the matter closes

The default is a fixed period, stated in the engagement letter, running from the final invoice. Unless you instruct otherwise in writing, it is twelve months.

Extension is available on written instruction at the published storage rate. Where material I hold is or may be subject to a litigation hold, a preservation order or an open regulatory matter, I keep holding it and say so, whatever the schedule would otherwise allow.

4.1

Destruction and the certificate

Nothing is destroyed without a written instruction from the retaining party, and the instruction has to identify the matter and the items. Once it is done I issue a certificate of destruction recording the items, the identifiers and digests they were held under, the method used, the date and time, and who carried it out. It is signed. It is issued whether or not anyone asks for it.

The certificate covers the copies I held. It is not a statement about copies held by you, by the opposing party, by a platform provider or by anyone else, and it is worded so that it cannot be read as one.

Certified destruction $150 per matter. The certificate is not charged separately.

5

Subpoenas and other legal process for material I hold

Material held for your matter can attract process of its own: a subpoena to me as a non-party, a preservation letter, a regulator's demand, a search warrant. How that gets handled is settled here, in advance, so that it is not improvised against somebody else's deadline.

5.1

What happens on receipt

Where notice is lawful, I notify the retaining party as soon as I am able, and I treat the compliance date on the process as my own deadline for doing it. That is the position in the ordinary civil case. It is not the position where a court has sealed the process or a statute forbids disclosure, and there I say what I can as soon as I am permitted to say anything at all. I do not volunteer material, I do not comply ahead of the date for compliance, and I do not treat a subpoena addressed to me as a licence to discuss the matter with anyone, least of all the party who sent it.

A subpoena to a non-party to produce documents or electronically stored information may be met with a written objection under Fed. R. Civ. P. 45(d)(2)(B), served before the earlier of the time specified for compliance or fourteen days after the subpoena is served. That window is short. The objection is served by me as the recipient, but whether one is served at all is your decision and your counsel's; my part is to get the document to you inside the window, to identify what I hold that falls within its terms, and to preserve everything in scope from the moment the process arrives.

Whether to object, to move to quash or to modify, and on what grounds, including privilege, work product and the protections for draft reports and attorney–expert communications under Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(4)(B) and 26(b)(4)(C), is counsel's call. I do not make it for you. Where a court orders production over your objection, I comply with the order and tell you exactly what went out.

Time spent responding to third-party process is charged at the published hourly rate, to the party whose material it is, with the usual written ceiling. Better you read that here than meet it on an invoice.

6

Subcontracting

The default is that nothing is subcontracted. Where a matter needs a capability I do not hold, a specialist extraction or a laboratory process or a hosted review platform or a certified or licensed examiner for your venue, I name the intended provider, say what they would receive and what they would do with it, and obtain your written consent before anything leaves my custody. Consent is sought in advance and never ratified afterwards. A refusal is a perfectly reasonable answer, and it may mean I decline the matter.

Anyone engaged under this clause is bound by these handling terms, is entered in the custody record as a named custodian, and goes through the same conflicts check I do. Material does not leave the jurisdiction without your written consent naming the destination.

7

Suspected child sexual abuse material — work stops immediately

This rule is absolute. An engagement letter cannot vary it, and neither can an instruction, a protective order or a client's preference.

If material that appears to be child sexual abuse material is encountered at any point — in triage, during an acquisition, during examination, in a carved artefact, in a thumbnail cache — examination stops at that moment. The material is not opened further, not copied, not extracted, not exported into a report, not attached to an email, and not shown to the client. Any image or container already in my custody is isolated and secured in place. I then contact the retaining party's counsel and, on counsel's instruction and without avoidable delay, law enforcement.

This is not squeamishness and it is not house policy. Knowing possession is itself an offence under 18 U.S.C. § 2252 and § 2252A, and there is no forensic exception for a private examiner. Copying it to preserve it. Viewing more of it to be sure. Carrying it to another facility to look at it properly. Each of those is an independently unlawful act, and an examiner who does them has committed an offence while trying to be diligent.

In federal criminal matters, 18 U.S.C. § 3509(m) keeps such material in the care, custody and control of the Government or the court, and directs the court to deny a defence request to copy or otherwise reproduce it for so long as the Government makes it reasonably available. The section defines that as ample opportunity for inspection, viewing and examination at a Government facility, by the defendant, defence counsel, and anyone the defendant seeks to qualify to give expert testimony. So the route runs through counsel and the court, at a Government facility, on the court's terms. Not a copy in my store.

Clients hear this before they hire me rather than after. If the rule engages mid-matter, work stops, the balance of any authorisation is unspent, and the engagement may end there.

8

Regulated and restricted data

Engagements touching protected health information, financial data under the Gramm–Leach–Bliley Act, personal data under the GDPR or UK GDPR, controlled unclassified information, or data subject to a protective order carry handling constraints of their own. Those are identified at scoping and written into the engagement letter: any business associate agreement, data processing terms, transfer mechanism, or restriction on where the data may be processed. All of it is settled before collection. A constraint discovered after an image exists is a constraint that has already been breached.

9

What these terms do not do

This page describes handling. It is worth saying plainly what handling does not buy, because a custody record gets offered as though it settled questions it never touches.

The handling commitments and their limits
Commitment What this does not establish
Documented chain of custody from receipt to disposal That the evidence is admissible, or that its authenticity will not be contested. Authentication is built at collection time and decided by the court; a custody record supports the foundation, it does not supply the ruling
Hash verification at acquisition and re-verification on every withdrawal That the source was unaltered before acquisition, or that any person did anything. Integrity and attribution are different questions and only one of them is answered by arithmetic
Encryption at rest, access control, and a logged access list That the store is accredited or inspected, or that I hold any certification, licence or laboratory accreditation. Where an engagement requires one, it is named in the engagement letter and met before work begins, or the matter is declined
Notice to the retaining party on receipt of legal process, where lawful A right to resist process on your behalf, or legal advice about whether to object. I preserve, I notify, I give you the window; the decision and the motion are counsel's
Certified destruction on written instruction That no copy survives elsewhere. The certificate speaks only to the copies I held and is written so that it cannot be read as saying more
Retention for the stated period, extended on instruction Advice on how long you are obliged to retain anything. Limitation periods, appeal windows and regulatory duties are yours and your counsel's to determine
10

Where these terms sit

The handling terms above are attached to the engagement letter and are read with the method, the rate schedule and the refusals. If a term on this page and a term in a signed engagement letter differ, the engagement letter governs. The difference will have been negotiated in the open rather than arrived at by silence.
