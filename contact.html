1

What this page is

This is the shortest document on the site, and the brevity is deliberate rather than economical. A contact page for a forensic practice has one job: to get a real person on a telephone quickly, having collected the minimum needed to check that we are free to speak to you at all. Everything beyond that minimum is either premature or actively harmful to the person filling it in.

So the form below asks for five things — your name, your role in the matter, whether counsel is already engaged, a number to call you back on, and an email address. Each field carries its reason in the field itself. There is no field for what happened, no file upload, no drop-down of incident types, and no box headed tell us about your matter. Clause 1.1 sets out why.

If something is happening right now, do not use the form. Use the telephone number in clause 2, and read the first-hour page while you wait for the call to connect. That page is written to be useful whether or not you ever speak to us.

1.1

Why there is no box for case detail

Privileged or sensitive facts should not be typed into a web form, and the reason is not squeamishness about data handling. It is that at the moment you would be typing them, nothing protects them.

An enquiry arrives before a conflicts check has been run and before an engagement letter is signed. Until both of those are done there is no engagement, no retention, and no structure through which a privilege claim could sensibly be made over what you sent. The material is simply a message from a member of the public to a business, sitting in a database and in a mailbox with nothing in place to protect it, and potentially a document that has to be produced and explained later by the person who wrote it.

There is a second reason, and it runs the other way. If a conflicts check shows that we cannot act — because the party on the other side has already spoken to us, or is an existing client — then the substantive detail you sent is detail we should never have received. We would have to sequester it, decline, and be careful for the rest of the matter. Opposing parties do sometimes approach specialists early precisely to put them out of reach, and a form that invites facts makes that manoeuvre cheap and makes us less useful to everybody.

The honest sequence is: identify yourself, let the conflicts check clear, then have the conversation on a telephone with someone who can tell you what to preserve before anything else is discussed. That conversation costs nothing and it is described in clause 3.2.

Do not attach anything, and do not send a device, an image, a log export or a single file until the conflicts check has cleared and the engagement letter is signed.

1.2

There is no chat widget on this site, and there will not be one

A chat bubble in the corner of a page invites a caller to type privileged facts into a third-party service. That is the whole objection. The visitor is not thinking about where the message goes; they are thinking about the thing that has gone wrong. What actually happens is that the substance of a legal matter is typed into a vendor's platform, stored on that vendor's infrastructure under that vendor's retention policy, and possibly processed for the vendor's own purposes, before anybody has checked whether we can act.

The same reasoning explains the rest of what is absent here. There is no analytics-driven callback prompt, no exit-intent capture, and no live-availability indicator. A green dot that says we are online is a response commitment made by a script, and this practice publishes no response commitment outside a signed standby agreement. Clause 3.1 sets that out.

Call 516-269-1517 and open the first-hour page in the meantime. Containment and preservation are two different jobs, and almost every avoidable loss in the first hour comes from doing containment in a way that also does destruction. 3

When the telephone is answered

No response interval and no staffed hours are published here, and none on the first-hour page either. A published figure is a promise made to everyone who reads it, and outside a signed agreement it is not staffed. What exists without one is that we answer when we can, and that we say so here rather than leave you to discover it during an incident. Ordinary enquiries — the form below, or an email — are dealt with in working hours rather than overnight. Clause 3.1 sets out the rest.

3.1

This is not a response commitment, and here is what would be

The paragraph above describes what happens, not how quickly. It is deliberately not written as an interval, because a one-principal practice cannot staff a guarantee at three in the morning, and a missed published response during an incident is a worse first impression than no published figure at all.

A stated response commitment exists only under a signed standby agreement, where it is set out in that agreement. Without one there is not, and we would rather say that now than at 02:00.

What we will do without any agreement in place is tell you quickly whether we can take the matter — including saying no immediately rather than holding it while you wait. Where we cannot act we say so, and where we can, we name someone who might. A fast no is worth more to you in the first hour than a slow maybe, and it costs nothing.

3.2

What the first call costs

Nothing. The scoping and preservation triage call is up to 45 minutes at no charge, and it includes a written preservation instruction where one is needed. There is also no charge for the call that ends with us saying the data cannot answer your question, or that you do not need a forensic examiner at all. Those two calls are not a loss leader; they are the two calls where the advice is most likely to be worth something, and billing for them would put a price on telling you the truth.

What the call is not is the beginning of an engagement. No work starts, no date is held and no file is accepted until the conflicts check has cleared and the engagement letter is signed. Rates are published in full at /rates before you speak to anyone, so the first call is about whether the work is worth doing rather than about what it will cost.

4

The enquiry form

Five fields. The reason for each is printed beside it, in the form itself, so that nobody has to trust a privacy page about what the field is for.

Asked so the conflicts check can be run. That check is the only thing that can happen before anything else, and it cannot be run against an anonymous enquiry.
Asked because it decides who the client would be, who instructions come from, and who the report belongs to. Those four roles are set out at /roles.
Asked because it changes who the first call should be with. Where counsel is engaged, the call is usually with them; where counsel is not engaged, the first thing we are likely to say is that one should be.
Asked because the first useful exchange is a telephone call, not a thread. It is also the channel on which it is safe to describe the matter, which this form is not.
Asked to confirm receipt, and to send the engagement letter if the matter gets that far. Treated as an administrative channel only, for the reason in clause 2.
There is deliberately no field for the matter itself. Please do not put case detail, custodian names, account identifiers or credentials into any field above.

5

What happens after you send it

The sequence is short and it is the same every time. The enquiry is read. The conflicts check is run against the parties you name, and against counsel on both sides where you can give them. If it clears, you get a call at the number you gave, and that call is the scoping call in clause 3.2 — free, up to forty-five minutes, and the right place to describe what happened. If preservation steps are needed before anything else, they are given on that call and confirmed in writing afterwards, whether or not you go on to instruct us.

If the check does not clear, you are told that we cannot act, promptly and without a substantive discussion having taken place. We will not be able to tell you why, and that discretion is the same protection you would be relying on if the position were reversed.

If the matter is one we should not take for any other reason — the deadline cannot be met, the question needs a discipline we do not practise, or the work belongs with a larger consultancy — we say so on the first call rather than accepting it and discovering it later. Where we can name someone better suited, we do.

5.1

If you are contacting us about a matter we are already in

Use the telephone number, say the engagement reference, and do not use this form. An existing matter has a named channel and a distribution list set out in its engagement letter, and routing correspondence about it through a public web form is how material ends up outside that list. If you are the opposing party, or counsel for the opposing party, in a matter we are already instructed in, contact should come through counsel rather than to us directly.

6

What we will not do with what you send

We will not

  1. Start work, hold a date, or accept a single file before the conflicts check clears and the engagement letter is signed. A payment made before that point is an offer, held unapplied, and refunded in full.
  2. Use what you send for anything other than the conflicts check, the callback, and the administration of an engagement if one follows. No list is sold, rented or shared, and there is no marketing sequence attached to this form.
  3. Disclose that you approached us, or what was said, to anyone — including to a party the conflict runs to. A preliminary approach is logged for conflicts purposes and is otherwise confidential whether or not we are instructed.
  4. Take or pay a referral fee, in either direction, for sending you anywhere else. Where we name someone better suited to your matter, no money moves between us.
  5. Put a chat widget, a live-availability indicator or a callback prompt on this site. Each of them invites the substance of a legal matter into a third-party service before anyone has checked whether we can act.
  6. Promise you a finding, a recovered file, an unlocked handset, or that anything we produce will be admitted. Admissibility is the court's decision and no one can guarantee it.

The full list is published on the home page and repeated verbatim in the engagement letter.
