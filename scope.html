How to read this page

This is the whole list, including the parts of it we do not perform. Capability boundaries live here, on the scope page, and never among the refusals. The distinction matters and it is deliberate. We do not take a commission from any vendor we place is a refusal of money — a principle, and it belongs on the refusals list at /#we-will-not. We do not perform chip-off extraction is a scope boundary: a statement about capacity, not about conduct. Moving it into the refusals would convert a principle into an admission of inexperience, and would quietly weaken the list that has to survive cross-examination intact.

So the refusals page tells you what we will not do for money. This page tells you what a one-principal practice can and cannot carry. Both are published because you are entitled to know which one you are reading.

1.1

The three price classes

Every priced line below carries one of three classes, abbreviated in the ledger and defined here in full. Where a fixed price attaches to a unit whose quantity is not knowable in advance — a gigabyte, an imaged page, a user-month — the ledger marks the line FIXED UNIT: the unit price is published and no total is, because the quantity is not ours to know until the work that produces it is done. Where the figure itself cannot honestly be published in advance, the ledger says Quoted or Not published in place of it, and the reason is stated in the same row rather than left for the call.

1.2

Take, refer out, decline

Each line also carries a position. TAKE means this practice performs the work. REFER OUT means the work is real, we understand it well enough to specify it and to read the output, and we do not perform it here — we will name someone who does, take no fee for the introduction in either direction, and where it helps we will work alongside them. DECLINE means we do not do it at all and would not arrange it.

A few of the declined lines are declined on principle rather than for want of capacity — acting for both sides, selling the remediation we were paid to investigate, collecting without documented authority. Those appear here because this ledger is the complete one, and they appear again, in full and as principles, on the refusals list. Nothing travels in the other direction: a capability this practice does not carry is never dressed up as a refusal.

A referral out is not a soft refusal. A one-principal practice that claims enterprise-scale capacity is making the first misrepresentation of the engagement, and it is the one opposing counsel finds first.

1.3

Tools, certifications and licensing

Tools are named on this site only where the name is material to understanding a method — a targeted collector of the KAPE or Velociraptor class, a hardware write blocker of the kind tested under the NIST CFTT programme, a commercial mobile extraction suite. A tool is named as a category example. It is not a claim to a current licence for that product, and it is not an endorsement. The tool and version actually used is recorded in the examination notes and stated in the report, which is where a tool claim can be checked rather than advertised. There is no logo wall on this site because we take no vendor commissions.

Where an engagement requires a certified examiner, an accredited laboratory, or a licence, that condition is identified at scoping, stated in the engagement letter, and satisfied before work begins — by associating an examiner who holds it, or by declining the matter. Private investigator licensing varies by state and digital examiners are not clearly carved out everywhere. We check the position for your venue and either hold the licence, work under one, or decline.

Cloud and SaaS

Cloud sources cannot be write-blocked. Soundness comes instead from documenting the method, recording the tool and version, hashing the export at the earliest possible point, and recording the authority relied on and the tenant's retention and licence tier at the moment of collection — because both change, and a log that was never enabled cannot be recovered afterwards.

What the ledger does not include

No line on this page carries a minimum-hours charge. The only minimums published anywhere on this site are the testimony ones, each with its reason stated: a reserved day cannot be resold. Published figures elsewhere in this field include ten hours per drive and six hours per phone; a minimum that quietly converts a two-hour job into a ten-hour invoice reads badly when the invoice becomes an exhibit.

We publish no typical-hours figure either. It would be a claim about past matters that you could not check. What we publish instead is the opening authorisation we would propose for your matter, before you commit to it.

Three things are never charged. The scoping and preservation triage call — up to 45 minutes, with a written preservation instruction if one is needed. The call that ends with us saying the data cannot answer your question. The call that ends with us saying you do not need a forensic examiner at all.

Our method is written against the four principles in ISO/IEC 27037:2012 — auditability, repeatability, reproducibility, justifiability — and against NIST SP 800-86. That is guidance we work to, not accreditation we hold; this is not an ISO/IEC 17025 accredited laboratory. No line on this page should be read as a promise of a finding, a recovered file, an unlocked handset, or that anything produced will be admitted. Admissibility is the court's decision and no one can guarantee it.
