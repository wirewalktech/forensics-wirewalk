How to read this page

This is the whole list, including the parts of it I do not perform. Capability boundaries live here, on the scope page, and never among the refusals. That distinction sounds like housekeeping until you put the two lists side by side. I do not take a commission from any vendor I place is a refusal of money. It is a principle, and it belongs on the refusals list at /#we-will-not. I do not perform chip-off extraction is something else: a statement about capacity, not about conduct. Move it across and the principle becomes an admission of inexperience, which weakens the list that has to survive cross-examination intact.

The refusals list tells you what I will not do for money. This page tells you what a one-principal practice can and cannot carry. Both are published because you are entitled to know which of the two you are reading.

1.1

The three price classes

Every priced line below carries one of three classes. They are abbreviated in the ledger and set out here in full. Some units have a price but no knowable quantity: a gigabyte, an imaged page. Those lines are marked FIXED UNIT, which means the unit price is published and the total is not, because until the work is done the quantity is not mine to know. Where the figure itself cannot honestly be published in advance, the row says Quoted or Not published and gives the reason there, instead of saving it for the call.

1.2

Take, refer out, decline

Each line also carries a position. TAKE means I do the work. REFER OUT means the work is real and I understand it well enough to specify it and to read the output, but I do not perform it here. I will name someone who does, take no fee for the introduction in either direction, and work alongside them where that helps. DECLINE means I do not do it at all, and would not arrange it either.

A few of the declines are principle rather than capacity: acting for both sides, selling the remediation I was paid to investigate, collecting without documented authority. They appear here because this ledger is meant to be the complete one, and they appear again as principles on the refusals list. Nothing travels the other way: a capability I do not carry is never dressed up as a refusal.

A referral out is not a soft no. If I claimed enterprise-scale capacity I would be making the first misrepresentation of the engagement, and it is the one opposing counsel finds first.

1.3

Tools, certifications and licensing

I name a tool on this site only where the name helps you understand a method: a targeted collector of the KAPE or Velociraptor class, a hardware write blocker of the kind tested under the NIST CFTT programme, a commercial mobile extraction suite. A tool is named as a category example. It is not a claim to a current licence for that product, and it is not an endorsement. The tool and version actually used goes into the examination notes and into the report, which is where a tool claim can be checked instead of advertised. There is no logo wall here, and I take no vendor commissions.

Where a matter requires a certified examiner, an accredited laboratory or a licence, that condition is identified at scoping, written into the engagement letter, and satisfied before any work begins. Either I associate an examiner who holds it, or I decline the matter. Private investigator licensing varies by state, and digital examiners are not clearly carved out everywhere. I check the position for your venue, and then either hold the licence, work under one, or decline.

Mobile devices

A handset model that gave up its full file system last year can give up very little after an OS update. Mobile is where overclaiming is easiest and where it does the most damage, because extraction levels are not interchangeable and none of them is guaranteed to work. So every extraction line below is priced per attempt rather than per result, and the level actually reached is stated in writing. This is the least welcome paragraph on the page, and I would rather you read it here than hear it from me on the phone after an attempt has already failed.

4

Cloud and SaaS

You cannot write-block a cloud account. Soundness has to come from somewhere else: documenting the method, recording the tool and version, hashing the export at the earliest point I can reach it, and writing down the authority I relied on together with the tenant's retention and licence tier at the moment of collection. Those last two change, sometimes while a matter is running. And a log that was never switched on cannot be recovered afterwards.

9

What the ledger does not include

No line on this page carries a minimum-hours charge. The only minimums anywhere on this site are the testimony ones, and the reason is printed beside them: a reserved day cannot be resold. Elsewhere in this field you will see ten hours per drive, or six hours per phone. A minimum that quietly turns a two-hour job into a ten-hour invoice reads badly when the invoice becomes an exhibit.

There is no typical-hours figure here either. It would be a claim about past matters that you have no way to check, and it would tell you nothing about yours. You get the opening authorisation I would propose for your matter instead, before you commit to anything.

Three things are never charged. The scoping and preservation triage call, up to 45 minutes, with a written preservation instruction if one is needed. The call that ends with me saying the data cannot answer your question. And the call that ends with me saying you do not need a forensic examiner at all.

My method is written against the four principles in ISO/IEC 27037:2012 — auditability, repeatability, reproducibility, justifiability — and against NIST SP 800-86. That is guidance I work to, not accreditation I hold: this is not an ISO/IEC 17025 accredited laboratory. No line on this page is a promise of a finding, a recovered file, an unlocked handset, or that anything I produce will be admitted. Admissibility is the court's decision and no one can guarantee it.
