1

Why this page exists

A fee that moves is a fee that can be cross-examined. An examiner whose rate depends on the result, on the side, or on the strength of the finding has handed opposing counsel a line of questioning that has nothing to do with the evidence and everything to do with the invoice. So the schedule is published, in full, before any conversation about your matter.

The rate is the same whether you are plaintiff or defendant, whether you retain us to support a case or to test one, and whether what we find helps you or does not. A null result is billed exactly like a helpful one. That is a worse outcome for us than the alternative and it is the only arrangement under which our numbers are worth anything to you.

This page is the commercial half of the site. The capability half is on scope, where every line carries what it does not establish, and the terms half is on engagement.

1.1

The three price classes

Every line on this page and on the service ledger is labelled with one of three classes.

Price classes
ClassWhat it meansWhat it does not establish
Fixed A stated price for a stated unit. The unit is named on the line — per device, per custodian, per copy, per matter. A fixed price for an artefact does not price the answer. Imaging a drive at a fixed price says nothing about what the examination of that image will cost.
Hourly against an authorised ceiling A published rate plus a written stop-point. No hour above the ceiling is billed. At 80% consumption we stop and report; work resumes only on a written increase. A ceiling is not an estimate of what the work will cost and not a promise that the question can be answered inside it. It is the point at which we stop and put the position in front of you.
Scoped after paid triage A fixed, time-boxed first block, credited in full against whatever follows it. The block buys a scoped proposal and whatever was collected inside it. It does not buy a conclusion, and it does not commit either of us to the work that follows.
1.2

The rule that decides which class applies

A fixed price buys an artefact of defined extent — an image, an extraction, a copy, a hash set, a certificate. Analysis cannot be priced before someone has looked at the artefact, because the extent of an answer is not knowable in advance. That is why some lines below carry a price and some carry a rate and a ceiling.

A flat number quoted for an examination is either pricing a very small examination or pricing something that will have to be revisited later. We would rather publish the rate and the stop-point than a number we would have to revisit at the point where you are least able to change examiner.

1.3

What this schedule does not have

No cart. Nothing on this site can be bought by clicking. Work begins after a conflicts check clears and an engagement letter is signed, and not before.

No discount, no promotion, no introductory rate, no volume tier and no seasonal adjustment. A rate that can be discounted is a rate that can be argued to have been discounted for a reason, and the reason a deposition suggests is never a flattering one. Two things are published that reduce what you pay, and both are stated in advance and applied on the same terms to everyone: the retained rate under a standby agreement, which is fixed a year before any matter arrives, and the bank-transfer discount against the card price set out on engagement, which is a payment-method term and does not move the rate itself.

No minimum hours on examination or collection work, of any kind. Minimums of ten hours per drive and six hours per phone are published elsewhere in this field; we do not carry them, because a minimum that quietly converts a two-hour job into a ten-hour invoice reads badly when the invoice becomes an exhibit. The only minimum on this page is the testimony minimum at clause 4, and it is published with its reason.

No charge for the scoping call, and specifically no charge for the call that ends with us saying the data cannot answer the question you have, or that you do not need a forensic examiner at all. It is the only service on this schedule we do not charge for.

No published figure for typical hours on a matter. It would be a claim about past engagements, which this site does not make. What we publish instead is the opening authorisation we would propose, given at scoping, in writing, for your matter.

This describes when the uplift applies if we do. Whether we can start, and how quickly, is answered on the call; a response commitment exists only under a signed standby agreement.

The uplift applies only to the out-of-hours hours, not to all time on the matter. An engagement that starts at 22:00 on a Thursday does not become a 35% matter. The hours worked that night carry the uplift; the hours worked the following Tuesday afternoon do not.

The published range in this field runs from 25% to 50%, usually attached to a trigger vague enough that the uplift is effectively at the examiner's discretion. It is among the most disputed lines on a forensics invoice. A fixed percentage with a clock-based trigger removes the argument, and it removes it in the direction that costs us money on a busy week.

The emergency mobilisation block at clause 5 is inclusive of the uplift. Under a standby agreement the uplift is waived for the first 48 hours of an incident.

2.2

Rate versioning

The rate in effect at the date of the engagement letter is the rate that applies to that matter. It is held for 12 months and changes only on 60 days' written notice. Work already authorised at a given rate bills at the rate authorised, including work authorised before a change and performed after it.

The version and revision dates of this document are in the header above. This schedule is reviewed at least once a year, which follows from holding a rate for 12 months, and every prior version stays at a stable URL under versions, so a rate quoted to you months ago can be checked against what was published on the day it was quoted rather than against what is published today.

4.1

Cancellation, and why a reserved day cannot be resold

Cancellation tiers
ReleasedChargeReason
More than 3 business days before the reserved dateNo chargeThree days is enough notice to place other work into the day.
Inside 3 business days50%Part of the day is recoverable, part is not.
Inside 1 business day100%None of it is recoverable. The day is gone whether or not anyone is deposed in it.

The reason is mechanical rather than commercial. A reserved testimony day is closed to everything else the moment it is held, and what it displaces is examination work, which cannot be started and stopped around a court's calendar. Once a cancellation is inside a day, that day cannot be sold to anybody, and a schedule that pretends otherwise ends up charging for it in some less visible way.

The same structure is why the four-hour minimum exists on a remote deposition: half a working day is displaced by a two-hour remote appearance as surely as by a four-hour one. That is the only minimum on this page.

Testimony is never capped. The schedule belongs to the court, not to us and not to you. A not-to-exceed on testimony is a promise no examiner can keep, and an examiner who has given one has a financial reason to want the day to end. Every other line on this page can carry a written ceiling; this one cannot, and we would rather say why than quietly leave it off.

Conditions attached to this schedule

Payment terms, retainer mechanics, authorisation ceilings, invoicing format and the conflicts procedure are on engagement and form part of the same letter as this schedule. Three conditions belong here because they are about the money on this page.

Expenses are billed at cost with no mark-up and with receipts. We take no commission from any hardware, software, platform or hosting vendor we place, and we do not sell you the remediation we were paid to investigate.

Where an engagement requires a certified examiner, an accredited laboratory, or a licence, that condition is identified at scoping, stated in the engagement letter, and satisfied before work begins — by holding it, by working under someone who holds it, or by declining the matter. We check the licensing position for your venue before we quote, because a fee schedule agreed for work that cannot lawfully be performed is worse than no schedule at all.

Nothing is started, no date is held and no file is accepted before the conflicts check clears and the engagement letter is signed. A payment made before that point is an offer. It is held unapplied and refunded in full if we do not take the matter, with no deduction for card processing.

8.1

The fee refusals

These are reproduced verbatim from the full list at we will not and appear word for word in the engagement letter.

We will not

  1. Take a fee that depends on what we find, on the outcome of the matter, or on which side retains us. The rate is the same either way and it is set before we look at anything. An examiner paid on the result is impeachable, and the client is the one who pays for that.
  2. Take or pay a referral fee, in either direction — to counsel, brokers, insurers, or anyone else who sends us work.
  3. Take a commission from any hardware, software, platform or hosting vendor we place, or sell you the remediation we were paid to investigate.
  4. Bill an hour above the ceiling you authorised in writing, or run an open-ended password recovery or extraction attempt without a written cap agreed first.
  5. Image a whole disk where a targeted collection answers the question, or bill you for expanded volume. Processing is measured on the data as we collected it.
  6. Tell you when culling would take your bill from five figures to four and then not do it — even though the bill we would otherwise send you is the larger one.
  7. Carry an overage or surge rate. Hours beyond a standby credit bill at the retained rate, not above it.
  8. Deduct card processing fees from an unearned balance we are returning to you.
