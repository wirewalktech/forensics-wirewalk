1

Why this page exists

A fee that moves is a fee that can be cross-examined. If my rate went up when the finding was helpful, opposing counsel would spend the first half hour of the deposition on my invoice instead of on the evidence, and that half hour would do more damage than anything in the report. So the whole schedule is here, before either of us has said a word about your matter.

The rate is the same whether you are plaintiff or defendant, and whether you retain me to support a case or to test one. It is also the same whether what I find helps you or is the last thing you wanted to hear. A null result is billed exactly like a useful one. That is obviously the worse outcome for me. It is also the only arrangement under which my numbers are worth anything to you.

This page is the money. What I can actually do is on scope, where every line carries what it does not establish. The terms are on engagement.

1.1

The three price classes

Every line here, and every line on the service ledger, carries one of three labels.

Price classes
ClassWhat it meansWhat it does not establish
Fixed A stated price for a stated unit. The unit is named on the line: per device, per custodian, per copy, per matter. A fixed price for an artefact does not price the answer. Imaging a drive at a fixed price says nothing about what examining that image will cost.
Hourly against an authorised ceiling A published rate plus a written stop-point. No hour above the ceiling is billed. At 80% consumption I stop and report; work resumes only on a written increase. A ceiling is not an estimate of what the work will cost, and it is not a promise that the question can be answered inside it. It is the point at which I stop and put the position in front of you.
Scoped after paid triage A fixed, time-boxed first block, credited in full against whatever follows it. The block buys a scoped proposal and whatever was collected inside it. It does not buy a conclusion, and it does not commit either of us to the work that follows.
1.2

The rule that decides which class applies

A fixed price buys an artefact of defined extent — an image, an extraction, a copy, a hash set, a certificate. Analysis cannot be priced before someone has looked at the artefact, because the extent of an answer is not knowable in advance. That is why some lines below carry a price and some carry a rate and a ceiling.

A flat number quoted for an examination is either pricing a very small examination or pricing something that will have to be reopened later. I would rather publish the rate and the stop-point. Reopening a price halfway through a matter is a conversation nobody enjoys, and you are the one in it with the least room to walk away.

1.3

What this schedule does not have

No cart. Nothing here can be bought by clicking. Work starts once the conflicts check has cleared and the engagement letter is signed, and not before.

No discount, no promotion, no introductory rate, no volume tier, no seasonal adjustment. A rate that can be discounted is a rate that can be argued to have been discounted for a reason, and the reason a deposition suggests is never a flattering one. Two things do reduce what you pay, and both are published in advance and applied the same way to everyone. One is the retained rate under a standby agreement, which is fixed a year before any matter arrives. The other is the bank-transfer discount against the card price set out on engagement, and that is a payment-method term rather than a change to the rate.

No minimum hours on examination or collection work, of any kind. Minimums of ten hours per drive and six hours per phone are published elsewhere in this field. I do not carry them. A minimum that quietly turns a two-hour job into a ten-hour invoice reads very badly when the invoice becomes an exhibit, and invoices do become exhibits. The only minimum on this page is the testimony minimum at clause 4, and its reason is printed next to it.

No charge for the scoping call. That includes the call that ends with me saying the data cannot answer your question, or that you do not need a forensic examiner at all. It is the only thing on this schedule I do not charge for.

No published figure for typical hours on a matter. That would be a claim about past engagements, and this site does not make those. What you get instead is the opening authorisation I would propose for your matter, in writing, at scoping.

After-hours and expedited uplift

There is one uplift, +35%, and one trigger for it. The trigger is written mechanically so that neither of us ends up arguing about it on an invoice.

Uplift trigger
ConditionEffectWhat this does not commit me to
Work performed outside 08:00–18:00 Eastern, Monday to FridayUplift applies to those hoursThat I am available outside those hours. The uplift prices out-of-hours work where it has been agreed. It is not an undertaking that out-of-hours work can be staffed on request.
Work performed on a Saturday, a Sunday or a US federal holidayUplift applies to those hoursAs above. Weekend availability is agreed matter by matter, in writing, and is not established by the existence of a weekend rate.
An engagement commencing within 24 hours of signatureUplift applies to the out-of-hours hours worked on itThat I can start within 24 hours of any signature. This says when the uplift applies if I do. Whether I can start, and how fast, is answered on the call; a response commitment exists only under a signed standby agreement.

The uplift applies only to the out-of-hours hours, not to all time on the matter. An engagement that starts at 22:00 on a Thursday does not become a 35% matter. The hours worked that night carry the uplift. The hours worked the following Tuesday afternoon do not.

Published uplifts in this field run from 25% to 50%, usually hung on a trigger vague enough that the uplift ends up being the examiner's to decide. It is one of the most disputed lines on a forensics invoice. A fixed percentage on a clock-based trigger settles the argument in advance, and it settles it in the direction that costs me money in a busy week.

The emergency mobilisation block at clause 5 is inclusive of the uplift. Under a standby agreement the uplift is waived for the first 48 hours of an incident.

2.2

Rate versioning

The rate in effect on the date of your engagement letter is the rate for your matter. I hold it for 12 months and change it only on 60 days' written notice. Work you have already authorised bills at the rate you authorised, including work authorised before a change and performed after it.

The version and revision dates are in the header above. Holding a rate for 12 months means reviewing this schedule at least once a year, and every superseded version stays at a stable URL under versions. So if I quoted you a number in March, you can check it against what this page said on the day I quoted it, rather than against what it says today.

4.1

Cancellation, and why a reserved day cannot be resold

Cancellation tiers
ReleasedChargeReason
More than 3 business days before the reserved dateNo chargeThree days is enough notice to place other work into the day.
Inside 3 business days50%Part of the day is recoverable, part is not.
Inside 1 business day100%None of it is recoverable. The day is gone whether or not anyone is deposed in it.

The reason is mechanical rather than commercial. The moment a testimony day is held it is closed to everything else, and what it displaces is examination work, which does not start and stop neatly around a court's calendar. Inside a day, that day cannot be sold to anybody. A schedule that pretends otherwise recovers the cost somewhere less visible.

It is the same reason a remote deposition carries a four-hour minimum. A two-hour remote appearance displaces half a working day as surely as a four-hour one does. That is the only minimum on this page.

Testimony is never capped. The schedule belongs to the court, not to me and not to you. A not-to-exceed on testimony is a promise no examiner can keep, and an examiner who has given one has a financial reason to want the day to end. Every other line on this page can carry a written ceiling. This one cannot, and I would rather say why than quietly leave it off.

Conditions attached to this schedule

Payment terms, retainer mechanics, authorisation ceilings, invoicing format and the conflicts procedure all live on engagement, and they form part of the same letter as this schedule. Three conditions belong here instead, because they are about the money on this page.

Expenses are billed at cost, with receipts and no mark-up. I take no commission from any hardware, software, platform or hosting vendor I place, and I will not sell you the remediation I was paid to investigate.

Where an engagement requires a certified examiner, an accredited laboratory or a licence, I identify that at scoping and state it in the engagement letter, and it is satisfied before work begins: either I hold it, or I work under someone who holds it, or I decline the matter. I check the licensing position for your venue before I quote. A fee schedule agreed for work that cannot lawfully be performed in your venue is worse than no schedule at all.

Nothing is started, no date is held and no file is accepted before the conflicts check clears and the engagement letter is signed. A payment made before that point is an offer. It is held unapplied, and refunded in full if I do not take the matter, with nothing deducted for card processing.

8.1

The fee refusals

These are the money ones, lifted word for word from the full list, and they appear in the engagement letter in these words too.

I will not

  1. Take a fee that depends on what I find, on the outcome of the matter, or on which side retains me. The rate is the same either way and it is set before I look at anything. An examiner paid on the result is impeachable, and the client is the one who pays for that.
  2. Take or pay a referral fee, in either direction, to counsel, brokers, insurers, or anyone else who sends me work.
  3. Take a commission from any hardware, software, platform or hosting vendor I place, or sell you the remediation I was paid to investigate.
  4. Bill an hour above the ceiling you authorised in writing, or run an open-ended password recovery or extraction attempt without a written cap agreed first.
  5. Image a whole disk where a targeted collection answers the question, or bill you for expanded volume. Processing is measured on the data as I collected it.
  6. Tell you when culling would take your bill from five figures to four and then not do it, even though the bill I would otherwise send you is the larger one.
  7. Carry an overage or surge rate. Hours beyond a standby credit bill at the retained rate, not above it.
  8. Deduct card processing fees from an unearned balance I am returning to you.
